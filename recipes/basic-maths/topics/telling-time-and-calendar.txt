Telling time, clocks and the calendar

Search terms: telling time, reading clocks, analog clock, digital clock, hour hand, minute hand, half past, quarter past, 24-hour time, calendar, days in a month.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

READING AN ANALOG CLOCK

An analog clock has a round face with the numbers 1 to 12 spaced evenly around it, and two main hands that turn clockwise:
- The short hand is the hour hand. It points to the current hour. It moves slowly, taking 12 hours to make one full rotation around the clock face.
- The long hand is the minute hand. It points to the minutes past the hour. It takes 60 minutes (1 hour) to go completely around the clock face once.
Each number on the clock face represents 5 minutes for the long minute hand: 1 means 5 minutes, 2 means 10 minutes, 3 means 15 minutes, 6 means 30 minutes, and 12 means 60 minutes (a new hour).

KEY CLOCK POSITIONS

- O'clock: When the long minute hand points straight up at 12. If the short hand points at 3, it is exactly 3:00 (three o'clock).
- Quarter past: When the minute hand points to 3 (15 minutes past the hour, one-quarter of the clock circle).
- Half past: When the minute hand points straight down at 6 (30 minutes past the hour, half of the circle).
- Quarter to: When the minute hand points to 9 (45 minutes past the hour, meaning 15 minutes remain before the next hour arrives).

DIGITAL CLOCKS: 12-HOUR AND 24-HOUR TIME

Digital clocks show time using digits separated by a colon, like 07:30.
- In 12-hour time, hours run from 1 to 12. We use AM for times between midnight and midday (morning), and PM for times between midday and midnight (afternoon and evening).
- In 24-hour time, the day starts at 00:00 (midnight) and counts up to 23:59. For times after 12:00 noon, simply add 12 to the afternoon hour: 1:00 PM becomes 13:00, 5:30 PM becomes 17:30, and 8:00 PM becomes 20:00.

UNITS OF TIME AND THE CALENDAR

Time is grouped into set astronomical cycles:
- 60 seconds = 1 minute
- 60 minutes = 1 hour
- 24 hours = 1 day (one full spin of the Earth on its axis)
- 7 days = 1 week
- 52 weeks = 1 year
- 12 months = 1 year (one complete orbit of the Earth around the Sun)
A regular year has 365 days. Every four years is a leap year with 366 days, adding an extra day (February 29) to keep the calendar aligned with Earth's orbit.
Months have 30 or 31 days, except February which has 28 days (29 in leap years). The knuckle trick helps you remember: make a fist; knuckles are 31-day months (January, March, May, July, August, October, December) and valleys between knuckles are shorter months.
