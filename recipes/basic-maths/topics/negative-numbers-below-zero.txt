Negative numbers and counting below zero

Search terms: negative numbers, below zero, minus numbers, cold temperature, thermometer, number line, subtracting past zero, debt.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT NEGATIVE NUMBERS ARE

Negative numbers are numbers that are less than zero. They are written with a minus sign (-) placed directly in front of the digit, like -1, -2, -3 and -10. On a horizontal number line, positive numbers lie to the right of zero, while negative numbers stretch out to the left of zero.

EVERYDAY EXAMPLES OF NEGATIVE NUMBERS

- Temperatures: On a cold winter day, when the air is freezing, a thermometer can drop below 0 °C to -5 °C (five degrees below zero).
- Buildings and lifts: Floors above the street level are positive (1, 2, 3), while underground basements or parking levels are often marked -1 or -2.
- Owing money (debt): If you want to buy a snack that costs 5 coins but you only have 3, you are short by 2 coins. If a friend lends you the 2 coins, your balance is -2 coins until you pay them back.

READING THE NUMBER LINE

Zero sits right in the middle between positive and negative numbers. Zero is neither positive nor negative.
As you move to the right on a number line, numbers get larger: -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3.
As you move to the left on a number line, numbers get smaller.
This means that -1 is actually larger than -5. Think of temperature: -1 °C is cold, but -5 °C is much colder and lower.

ADDING AND SUBTRACTING WITH NEGATIVE NUMBERS

When working on a number line:
- Adding a positive number means moving to the right (getting warmer or larger). Starting at -3 and adding 5 brings you past zero to +2.
- Subtracting a positive number means moving to the left (getting colder or smaller). Starting at 2 and subtracting 4 moves you left past zero to -2.
- Taking away 1 from 0 gives -1. Taking away 1 from -1 gives -2.
Picturing a thermometer or a person walking along a line makes moving across zero simple and visual.
