Not responding and not breathing normally, child

Search terms: child CPR, child not breathing, kid collapsed, child unconscious gasping.

DRAFT, NOT FOR EMERGENCY USE

Written from IFRC 2025. Not reviewed by a clinician.

For: Child

Age: Looks like a child: about one year or older, and not yet through puberty. If they look under about one year, use the baby card.

Not for: Baby under about one year or a teenager or adult. Use the card for their age.

USE THIS CARD WHEN

- Does not respond, and breathing is absent, gasping, noisy or irregular. If you are unsure whether breathing is normal, treat it as not normal.

GET HELP

Shout for help and have someone get emergency help now. Alone with a phone: call on speaker and start. Alone with no way to call while doing CPR: do CPR for one minute, then stop to get help, leaving if there is no phone nearby. Say clearly that the person is not responding and not breathing normally. The call taker may be able to guide you through CPR.

WHAT TO DO

1. Gently tap the shoulders and ask loudly whether they are all right. [p. 187]

2. Open the airway. Tilt the head back and lift the chin. [p. 187]

3. Spend up to 10 seconds watching the chest or belly, listening for breath and feeling for air on your cheek. Decide whether the breathing is normal. If unsure, treat it as not normal. [pp. 187, 188]

4. Get help as described above. [p. 188]

5. Give 30 chest compressions with one or two hands on the center of the chest. Use two hands if you are small or the child is large. Push fast and steadily, 100 to 120 times a minute, to one-third of the chest's depth, about 5 cm. [pp. 187, 188]

6. Give 2 breaths, mouth to mouth. Blow steadily for one second until the chest or belly rises. [p. 188]

7. Keep repeating 30 compressions and 2 breaths. [p. 188]

8. If you cannot or will not give breaths, keep doing compressions alone. Breaths matter a lot for children, so give them if you can. [pp. 186, 188]

9. Ask someone to fetch a defibrillator (AED) if one exists. If you are alone and can see one close by, you can leave to get it quickly. Under eight years old, a pediatric device or pads should be used. If there are none, a standard one could be used. From eight years old, use a standard one. Keep doing CPR until it is ready to analyze, then follow its voice prompts, interrupting CPR as little as possible. Restart compressions immediately after a shock. When the device checks the heart or gives a shock, make sure nobody is touching the person, and warn those around you. [pp. 188, 196, 197, 198]

10. If others are there, swap every one to two minutes with as little break as possible. [p. 188]

DO NOT

- Do not spend time feeling for a pulse. It is not needed. [p. 186]

- Do not give more or stronger breaths than needed, as the air goes into the stomach. [p. 190]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- While giving breaths, watch for movement or coughing. [p. 188]

- If the child does not respond but breathes normally, keep the airway open. Use the card for not responding and breathing normally. [p. 188]

- If the child recovers, keep watching closely until help takes over, because breathing can stop again. [p. 189]

WITH NO MEDICAL SUPPLIES

- The steps on this card use only your hands and mouth. Use a firm surface when possible. [pp. 187, 188]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

Does not apply to this card.

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep going until help arrives, or the child shows signs of life (coughing, opening eyes, speaking or moving on purpose) and starts breathing normally. [p. 188]

- Where no emergency service or higher care exists and CPR has not worked within the first 20 minutes, you could stop, as it is unlikely to succeed. This does not apply after drowning or severe cold. [p. 188]

- If you must carry the child from a remote place to care, keep doing CPR without stopping, on a firm surface. [p. 189]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- IFRC calls for emergency services and a defibrillator where available. The sources checked name nothing that replaces advanced care. [pp. 188, 189]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- IFRC notes that other guidance allows different ratios and orders, and either compressions or breaths first (pp. 188-189). This card follows IFRC's numbered steps.

- IFRC's numbered steps say that with no phone nearby you leave to get help as quickly as possible (p. 188). Another point on the same page says to do CPR for one minute first if you cannot call while doing it. This card combines them in that order.

LEFT OFF THIS CARD

- 15 compressions to 2 breaths. IFRC offers this ratio to trained responders. Untrained rescuers use 30 to 2.

- Starting with breaths. IFRC prefers breaths first in good practice but accepts compressions first, and its numbered steps start with compressions. The card follows the numbered steps.

- Pad placement on a child or baby. The device's prompts lead the rescuer.

Source: IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025. Page numbers in square brackets are pages of that source.
