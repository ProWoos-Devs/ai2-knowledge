Not responding, but breathing normally

Search terms: unconscious but breathing, passed out, recovery position, knocked out.

Written from IFRC 2025. Not reviewed by a clinician.

For: Adult, child and baby, with baby differences marked

Age: Go by how the person looks. A baby looks under about one year old.

Not for: Gasping, irregular, noisy or absent breathing: use the topic for not breathing normally.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The person does not respond to voice or touch and their breathing is normal

GET HELP

Always get emergency help for someone who does not respond, even if they breathe normally.

WHAT TO DO

1. Check for a response. Gently shake the shoulders and ask loudly whether they are all right. For a baby, tap the foot. [p. 153]

2. Before moving them, ask whether a spine injury is possible: a fall or dive from higher than standing height, a dive into shallow water, being crushed, a road collision, a sports accident, an explosion or mechanical accident, a painful injury, especially at the head or neck, or an obvious deformity of the head, neck or spine. In people over 65 or with a bone condition, even a minor slip or fall can cause one. Some people show no signs. [pp. 307, 308]

3. Open the airway: ease the head back until the mouth opens, then raise the chin. For a baby, bring the head only to a neutral position. If no spine injury is possible, turn them onto their back first if needed. If one is possible, try to do this as they lie, and move them only as much as you must. [pp. 153, 161, 309]

4. Spend up to 10 seconds watching the chest or belly, listening for breath and feeling for air on your cheek. Decide whether the breathing is normal. If you have any doubt, treat it as not normal and start CPR using the topic for their age. [pp. 153, 154, 159, 161, 188]

5. IF BREATHING IS NORMAL AND A SPINE INJURY IS POSSIBLE: leave them as you found them, keep the airway open as in step 3, and gently support the head to prevent movement. Move them only if they are in danger or help has arrived. If they must be moved, two people are best, one keeping the head in line with the spine. [pp. 153, 307, 309]

6. IF BREATHING IS NORMAL AND NO SPINE INJURY IS POSSIBLE: roll them onto their side and tilt the head back to keep the airway open. For a baby, use a neutral head position, and you can hold a baby this way in your arms. A pregnant person may be placed on their left side. [pp. 152, 153]

7. Get emergency help. [pp. 153, 154]

8. Keep watching their breathing and whether they respond. If you can, try to find out why this happened. [p. 153]

DO NOT

- Do not take longer than 10 seconds to check breathing. [p. 152]

- Do not move someone with a possible spine injury if help will arrive soon. [p. 152]

- Do not strap the head or neck, and do not use a neck collar. [p. 307]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If breathing stops, becomes noisy, turns to gasping or is barely there, start CPR at once using the topic for their age. [pp. 152, 154]

WITH NO MEDICAL SUPPLIES

- Every step on this topic is done with your hands. No equipment is needed. [p. 153]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- If you cannot roll the person onto their side, keep the airway open with the head tilt and chin lift. Limits: For when there is no risk of a spine injury. [p. 152]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep watching their breathing and response the whole time. Be ready to start CPR. [pp. 153, 154]

- For a possible spine injury with help far away: if at least two people are present, you can turn the person onto their side while keeping the spine straight. [pp. 152, 154]

- No guidance was found in the sources checked on how long to keep waiting if help will never come. IFRC's general advice for long waits is to keep watching for changes, keep them warm or cool as needed, prevent infection, and arrange transport if possible. [pp. 40, 121]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Not responding may mean a serious condition. The sources checked name nothing a bystander can do in place of medical care. [pp. 152, 153]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- IFRC notes that resuscitation councils differ slightly on when to call for help for an unconscious child or baby (p. 154). This topic follows IFRC's own steps.

- IFRC's two-person turn when help is far away (p. 154) is worded differently from its spinal injury section, which says to move only once help arrives or if the person is in danger (p. 307). This topic gives both.

- Turning for the airway check: IFRC's steps start with the person on their back (p. 153) and say you may need to turn them (p. 161), while its spinal section says to leave the person as found (p. 309). This topic leaves them as found where it can.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Jaw thrust. Our decision: the steps are not described for an untrained reader. IFRC says it may move the neck less than the head tilt.

- AVPU scale. Our decision: not needed for these steps. IFRC says no formal training is needed to use it.

Source: IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025. Page numbers in square brackets are pages of that source.
