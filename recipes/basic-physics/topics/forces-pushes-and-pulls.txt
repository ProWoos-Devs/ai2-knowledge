Forces, pushes and pulls

Search terms: forces, push and pull, balanced forces, unbalanced forces, Newtons, motion, speeding up, slowing down, changing direction.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT A FORCE IS

A force is a push or a pull acting upon an object. Forces are all around us, even when we cannot see them. Whenever you kick a football, push a heavy swing, pull open a drawer, or stretch a rubber band, you are applying a force. Forces are measured in units called Newtons (abbreviated as N), named after the English scientist Sir Isaac Newton.

WHAT FORCES DO TO OBJECTS

A force can cause an object to do several things:
- Start moving from a standstill (kicking a still ball).
- Speed up (pushing a bicycle pedals harder).
- Slow down or stop (pulling on the brakes).
- Change direction (hitting a tennis ball back across the net with a racket).
- Change shape (squeezing clay or squishing a sponge).

BALANCED FORCES

Balanced forces are forces acting on an object that are equal in size but push or pull in opposite directions. When forces are balanced, they cancel each other out completely.
- If an object is sitting still (like a book resting on a table), the forces are balanced: gravity pulls the book down, and the table pushes up with the exact same strength. The book does not move.
- If an object is already moving at a steady speed in a straight line, balanced forces keep it cruising along smoothly without speeding up or slowing down.

UNBALANCED FORCES

Unbalanced forces occur when one force is stronger than the forces opposing it. Unbalanced forces cause a change in motion:
- In a game of tug-of-war, when both teams pull with equal force, the rope does not budge (balanced).
- When one team pulls harder than the other, the forces become unbalanced, and the rope moves toward the stronger team.
Whenever an object accelerates, decelerates, or curves around a bend, an unbalanced force is at work.

CONTACT AND NON-CONTACT FORCES

Some forces require physical contact to work, such as your hand pushing a door or friction rubbing against shoes. Other forces can act across empty space without touching at all, called non-contact forces. The three main non-contact forces are gravity, magnetism, and static electricity.
