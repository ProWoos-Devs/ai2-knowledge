Gravity and why things fall

Search terms: gravity, gravitational pull, falling, why things fall down, weight and mass, Moon gravity, Isaac Newton, center of the Earth.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT GRAVITY IS

Gravity is an invisible pulling force that pulls objects toward one another. Every object in the universe that has mass exerts a gravitational pull on every other object. Because planet Earth is gigantic and has an enormous amount of mass, its gravity is very strong, pulling everything on or near Earth straight down toward the center of the planet.

WHY DROPPED OBJECTS FALL DOWN

When you let go of a ball or an apple, Earth's gravity pulls it downward until it hits the floor. Without gravity, objects would not fall; people, houses, oceans, and the atmosphere would float away into outer space. Gravity is what keeps the Moon in orbit around the Earth, and keeps the Earth in orbit around the Sun.

MASS VERSUS WEIGHT

Mass and weight are two different scientific concepts that people often mix up in everyday speech:
- Mass is the amount of physical matter (particles) that an object is made of. Mass is measured in kilograms (kg) and never changes, no matter where you travel in the universe. If your mass is 30 kg on Earth, your mass is still exactly 30 kg on the Moon or floating in space.
- Weight is the measure of the pulling force of gravity acting on your mass. Weight is measured in newtons (N). Because the Moon has much less mass than Earth, its gravity is only about one-sixth as strong. On the Moon, you would weigh one-sixth of what you weigh on Earth and could jump much higher, even though your body mass is unchanged.

DO HEAVIER THINGS FALL FASTER?

In pure gravity, with no air resistance, all objects speed up at the same rate as they fall, regardless of how heavy or light they are. If you drop a heavy steel hammer and a light feather in a tube where all the air has been pumped out (a vacuum), both hit the ground at the very same instant. In everyday air, a feather flutters down slowly only because air pushes against its wide, light surface (air resistance), not because light things fall more slowly.
