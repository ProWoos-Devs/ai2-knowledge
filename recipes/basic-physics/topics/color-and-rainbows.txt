Color and rainbows

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHITE LIGHT CONTAINS ALL COLORS

Sunlight and the light from everyday white lamps look colorless, but white light is actually a combination of all the colors of the rainbow blended together. In the 1660s, Sir Isaac Newton proved this by shining a narrow beam of sunlight through a triangular glass prism. The glass bent the light rays and spread them out into a brilliant fan of colors called the visible light spectrum.

THE COLORS OF THE SPECTRUM (ROYGBIV)

The colors of the rainbow always appear in the exact same order:
1. Red
2. Orange
3. Yellow
4. Green
5. Blue
6. Indigo
7. Violet
A popular mnemonic to memorize this order is the name "ROY G. BIV". Light travels as waves, and each color is light with a slightly different wavelength (the distance from one wave to the next). Red light has the longest wavelength and bends the least; violet light has the shortest wavelength and bends the most.

REFRACTION: BENDING LIGHT

Refraction is the bending of a light ray when it passes from one transparent medium into another, such as traveling from air into glass or water. Light travels slower in water and glass than in open air. When a light beam enters water at an angle, one side slows down before the other, causing the beam to pivot and change direction. This is why a straight drinking straw looks broken or bent when placed in a glass of water.

HOW RAINBOWS FORM IN THE SKY

A rainbow appears in the sky when sunlight shines through falling rain droplets while the Sun is behind you. Each tiny round raindrop acts as a miniature prism:
1. Sunlight enters the raindrop and bends (refracts).
2. The light separates into its constituent colors because each color bends at a slightly different angle.
3. The separated colors bounce off the inside back wall of the raindrop (internal reflection).
4. As the colors exit the front of the raindrop, they bend again and fan out into the sky, creating the giant colorful circular arc we admire.

WHY OBJECTS HAVE COLOR

An opaque object gets its color from the wavelengths of light it reflects into our eyes and the wavelengths it absorbs:
- A ripe red apple looks red because its surface absorbs blue, green, and yellow light, reflecting mostly red light back to your eyes.
- A green leaf absorbs most red and blue light, reflecting mostly green light.
- A pure white shirt reflects almost all colors of light equally.
- A black jacket absorbs almost all light rays, transforming them into heat energy (which is why black clothes feel warmer in direct sunshine).
