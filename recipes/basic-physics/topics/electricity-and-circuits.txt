Electricity and circuits

Search terms: electricity, electric circuits, batteries, bulbs, switches, conductors, electrical insulators, electrons, current, electrical safety.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT ELECTRICITY IS

Electricity is a form of energy caused by the flow of tiny, negatively charged subatomic particles called electrons. When electrons move along a pathway, they create an electric current, which can power lamps, spin electric motors, heat toasters, and run computers.

WHAT A COMPLETE CIRCUIT IS

An electric circuit is an unbroken, continuous loop through which electric current can flow. If there is any gap, break, or loose connection anywhere along the path, the loop is broken (an open circuit) and electricity instantly stops flowing.
A simple circuit contains four basic components:
1. Power source (like a battery): Provides the pushing force (voltage) that drives electrons through the circuit. A battery has two terminals: positive (+) and negative (-).
2. Conducting wires (usually copper metal wrapped in plastic): Provides the smooth roadway for electrons to travel.
3. Electrical load (like a small lightbulb, buzzer, or motor): Uses the electrical energy to produce light, sound, or movement.
4. Switch: A control gate that opens or closes the circuit safely. When the switch is closed (ON), the loop is complete and the bulb shines. When the switch is open (OFF), the gap blocks the current and the bulb turns off.

ELECTRICAL CONDUCTORS AND INSULATORS

- Electrical conductors: Materials that allow electricity to flow through them freely because their electrons move easily. Metals like copper, aluminum, silver, gold, and steel are excellent electrical conductors. Water containing dissolved minerals or salt also conducts electricity.
- Electrical insulators: Materials that strongly resist the flow of electric current because their electrons are tightly bound. Plastic, rubber, glass, ceramics, and dry wood are outstanding insulators. Wires are coated with flexible plastic insulation so you can handle them safely without getting a shock.

ELECTRICAL SAFETY

Electricity is immensely useful, but mains electricity from household wall sockets is extremely powerful and dangerous:
- Safe for experiments: Small, low-voltage AA or AAA batteries (1.5 volts) are safe for children to touch and build circuits with in class or at home. Always put a bulb or buzzer in the loop: a wire joined straight from one end of a battery to the other gets hot.
- Dangerous: Mains electricity (100 to 240 volts, depending on the country) can kill. Never touch wall sockets, never poke objects into sockets, and never use electrical cords near water or sinks.
