Electricity and circuits

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT ELECTRICITY IS

Electricity is about electric charges and the forces between them. Charges can build up on an object, which is static electricity, or flow along a path, which is an electric current. In metal wires the moving charges are electrons, tiny particles much smaller than an atom that carry a negative electric charge. A battery supplies the energy, and the current carries it around the circuit, which can power lamps, spin electric motors, heat toasters, and run computers.

WHAT A COMPLETE CIRCUIT IS

An electric circuit is an unbroken, continuous loop through which electric current can flow. If there is any gap, break, or loose connection anywhere along the path, the loop is broken (an open circuit) and electricity instantly stops flowing.
A simple circuit contains four basic components:
1. Power source (like a battery): Provides the push, called voltage, that drives electrons through the circuit. A battery has two terminals: positive (+) and negative (-).
2. Conducting wires (usually copper metal wrapped in plastic): Provides the smooth roadway for electrons to travel.
3. Electrical load (like a small lightbulb, buzzer, or motor): Uses the electrical energy to produce light, sound, or movement.
4. Switch: A control gate that opens or closes the circuit safely. When the switch is closed (ON), the loop is complete and the bulb shines. When the switch is open (OFF), the gap blocks the current and the bulb turns off.

ELECTRICAL CONDUCTORS AND INSULATORS

- Electrical conductors: Materials that allow electricity to flow through them freely because their electrons move easily. Metals like copper, aluminum, silver, gold, and steel are excellent electrical conductors. Water containing dissolved minerals or salt also conducts electricity.
- Electrical insulators: Materials that strongly resist the flow of electric current because their electrons are tightly bound. Plastic, rubber, glass, ceramics, and dry wood are outstanding insulators. Wires are coated with flexible plastic insulation to keep the electricity inside the wire. If a cord that plugs into the wall is cracked, frayed or has bare metal showing, do not touch it, and tell an adult.

ELECTRICAL SAFETY

Electricity is immensely useful, but mains electricity from household wall sockets is extremely powerful and dangerous:
- For experiments: With an adult, use an undamaged 1.5-volt AA or AAA battery in a holder, and a bulb or buzzer made for that voltage. Keep the circuit dry. Always put the bulb or buzzer in the loop and never join the two ends of a battery directly with a wire: the wire and the battery can get hot. If a battery or wire becomes hot, stop and tell the adult. Do not use leaking or damaged batteries. Never put any battery in your mouth. Small round button batteries are very dangerous if swallowed.
- Dangerous: Mains electricity (100 to 240 volts, depending on the country) can kill. Never touch wall sockets, never poke objects into sockets, and never use electrical cords near water or sinks.
