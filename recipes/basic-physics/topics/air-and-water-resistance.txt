Air and water resistance

WHAT AIR RESISTANCE IS

Air resistance (also called aerodynamic drag) is a type of friction caused by air pushing against a moving object. Even though air is invisible and feels like nothing, it is made of a huge number of tiny particles (molecules). When an object travels through the air, it has to push those air particles out of the way, and the particles push back, creating a resisting force that slows the object down.

HOW SPEED AND SHAPE AFFECT DRAG

The amount of air resistance depends on two key things:
1. The area facing the air: Wide, flat objects meet more air particles and experience much more drag. If you drop a flat sheet of paper, it catches the air and drifts down slowly. If you crumple the same sheet into a tight little ball, a much smaller area faces the air, so it meets much less drag and drops quickly to the floor.
2. Speed: The faster an object moves, the more air resistance pushes against it. You can feel this on a bicycle: when you ride slowly you feel a gentle breeze, but when you ride fast the air pushes hard against your face and chest.

HOW PARACHUTES WORK

A parachute uses a massive canopy of fabric to catch a huge volume of air as it drops. The air underneath pushes upward with strong air resistance. Very soon this push balances the downward pull of gravity, so the skydiver stops speeding up and comes down at a slow, steady speed instead of a dangerously fast one.

WHAT STREAMLINED MEANS

Streamlining is designing an object with a smooth, pointed, tapered shape so it slices cleanly through air or water with minimal drag.
- Nature: Fast-swimming sharks and dolphins have torpedo-shaped bodies that glide through water. Birds such as falcons fold their wings into sharp arrowheads when diving through the air.
- Human engineering: High-speed bullet trains, racing cars, jet planes, and cycling helmets are carefully shaped and tested in wind tunnels so they move faster while using less energy.

WATER RESISTANCE

Water is about 800 times denser than air, meaning its liquid molecules are packed much closer together. Pushing through water produces water resistance, which is much stronger than air resistance. Trying to run through knee-deep water at the beach feels heavy and exhausting because your legs are fighting powerful water drag.
