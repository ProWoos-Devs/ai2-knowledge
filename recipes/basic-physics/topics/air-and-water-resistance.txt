Air and water resistance

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT AIR RESISTANCE IS

Air resistance (also called aerodynamic drag) is a type of friction caused by air pushing against a moving object. Even though air feels invisible and weightless, it is filled with billions of tiny air particles (molecules). When an object travels through the air, it has to push those air particles out of the way, and the particles push back, creating a resisting force that slows the object down.

HOW SPEED AND SURFACE AREA AFFECT DRAG

The amount of air resistance depends on two key things:
1. Surface area: Wide, flat objects meet more air particles and experience huge drag. If you drop a flat sheet of paper, it catches the air and drifts down slowly. If you crumple the exact same sheet of paper into a tight little ball, it has a tiny surface area, encounters much less drag, and drops straight to the floor rapidly.
2. Speed: The faster an object moves, the more air resistance pushes against it. You can feel this by holding your hand out of an open car window: at low speeds you feel a gentle breeze, but at highway speeds the air pushes your hand back with great force.

HOW PARACHUTES WORK

A parachute uses a massive canopy of fabric to catch a huge volume of air as it drops. The air trapped underneath pushes upward with powerful air resistance that balances out the downward pull of gravity. This balance allows a skydiver to float gently and safely down to the ground rather than crashing.

WHAT STREAMLINED MEANS

Streamlining is designing an object with a smooth, pointed, tapered shape so it slices cleanly through air or water with minimal drag.
- Nature: Fast-swimming sharks and dolphins have torpedo-shaped bodies that glide through water. Birds such as falcons fold their wings into sharp arrowheads when diving through the air.
- Human engineering: High-speed bullet trains, racing cars, jet planes, and cycling helmets are carefully curved and streamlined in wind tunnels so they move faster while burning far less fuel.

WATER RESISTANCE

Water is about 800 times denser than air, meaning its liquid molecules are packed much closer together. Pushing through water produces water resistance, which is much stronger than air resistance. Trying to run through knee-deep water at the beach feels heavy and exhausting because your legs are fighting powerful water drag.
