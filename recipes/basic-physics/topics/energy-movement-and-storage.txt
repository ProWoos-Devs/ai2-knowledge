WHAT ENERGY IS

Energy is the ability to do work, cause movement, or create change in the physical world. Nothing can happen without energy: cars cannot drive, bulbs cannot shine, plants cannot grow, and your heart cannot beat without energy. Energy is measured in units called joules (abbreviated as J).

THE TWO MAIN FAMILIES OF ENERGY

Energy comes in many forms. Two broad families are a good place to start:
1. Kinetic energy: The energy of moving objects. Anything that is moving has kinetic energy. A rolling bowling ball, a flying bird, rushing river water, and the blowing wind all have kinetic energy. The faster an object moves and the more mass it has, the more kinetic energy it carries.
2. Potential energy: Energy that is stored and waiting to be released later.

COMMON TYPES OF STORED (POTENTIAL) ENERGY

- Gravitational potential energy: Energy stored in an object because of its height above the ground. A roller coaster car paused at the very peak of a steep hill has lots of gravitational potential energy. When it plunges downward, that stored energy transforms into rushing kinetic energy.
- Elastic potential energy: Energy stored in something that is stretched or squashed, like a pulled slingshot rubber band, a compressed mattress spring, or the wound-up spring inside a toy car. When the spring unwinds, its stored energy turns into the movement of the toy.
- Chemical energy: Energy stored in substances such as food, wood, coal, and the chemicals inside batteries. It is released when those substances react, for example when wood burns in air. When you eat an apple, your body digests it and releases chemical energy to power your muscles.

OTHER FORMS OF ENERGY YOU MEET EVERY DAY

- Thermal energy: The energy of the jiggling, moving particles inside everything, more of it in hot soup than in cold soup. When this energy passes from a hot thing to a cooler thing, we call it heat.
- Light energy: Radiant waves that travel from the Sun or lightbulbs and allow our eyes to see.
- Sound energy: Vibrating air waves made by speaking, clapping, or musical instruments.
- Electrical energy: Energy carried by an electric current from a source such as a battery to a device such as a lamp.

THE LAW OF CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

One of the most important rules of physics is the law of conservation of energy: Energy can never be created out of nothing, and energy can never be destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form into another.
For example, when you switch on a flashlight:
Stored chemical energy in the battery -> changes into electrical energy flowing in the wire -> changes into bright light energy and warm heat energy. The total amount of energy stays identical before and after.
