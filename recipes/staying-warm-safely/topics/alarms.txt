Carbon monoxide and smoke alarms

Search terms: carbon monoxide alarm, CO detector, smoke alarm, smoke detector, where to put the alarm, alarm battery, test the alarm, when to replace the detector, fire extinguisher.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: The alarms that warn of carbon monoxide and fire, and having an extinguisher ready

Not for: What to do when the carbon monoxide alarm sounds. See If you think it is carbon monoxide.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- You will heat with a fireplace, wood stove, kerosene heater or anything else that burns fuel
- You run a generator
- You want to check your alarms

WHAT TO DO

1. Use carbon monoxide alarms that run on batteries or have battery backup, so they work in a power cut. [CO, CPSC, Prepare]

2. Put one near every sleeping area, and one on every level of the home. [CO, CPSC]

3. If you heat with a fireplace, wood stove or kerosene heater, put a smoke alarm and a battery carbon monoxide alarm near the area being heated. [Prepare]

4. Test them every month and change the batteries twice a year. [Prepare]

5. Replace a carbon monoxide alarm as its maker says, or every 5 years. [CO]

6. Alarms that are connected to each other are best: when one sounds, they all sound. [CPSC]

7. Keep a multipurpose dry-chemical fire extinguisher nearby. [Prepare]

DO NOT

- Do not rely on an alarm that runs only on mains power during a power cut. The sources ask for battery or battery-backup alarms. [CO, CPSC]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside into fresh air at once. See If you think it is carbon monoxide. [CPSC]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- With no alarm, keep every fuel-burning device out of the home unless it is vented to the outside. See Never use these indoors. [CO, Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- CDC suggests an alarm with a digital readout, which shows the highest carbon monoxide level reached as well as sounding. [CO]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep testing the alarms every month and changing their batteries as the sources say, even when no help is near. [Prepare, CO]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Your senses cannot replace a carbon monoxide alarm. The gas has no color and no smell. [CO, CPSC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Battery checks: CDC's winter page says test monthly and change batteries twice a year; its carbon monoxide page says check them when the clocks change in spring and fall. This topic gives the monthly test.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- How to use a fire extinguisher, and what to do in a house fire. Not in these sources.
