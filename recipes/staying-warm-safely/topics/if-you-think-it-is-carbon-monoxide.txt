If you think it is carbon monoxide

Search terms: carbon monoxide poisoning what to do, CO alarm going off, alarm beeping, headache and dizzy near heater, someone passed out near generator, fumes in the house, get out, fresh air.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: When someone may be breathing carbon monoxide, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds

Not for: Preventing it. See Carbon monoxide and Never use these indoors.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- People have a headache, dizziness, weakness, sickness, vomiting, chest pain, sleepiness or confusion while something burns fuel indoors or nearby [CO, CPSC]
- A carbon monoxide alarm sounds. Treating the alarm as a reason to suspect poisoning is our reading; the sources say to install alarms but not what to do when one sounds.

WHAT TO DO

1. Get everyone outside into fresh air at once. [CPSC]

2. Then, from outside, get emergency help. [CPSC]

3. Once they are in fresh air, if a person does not respond, see "Not responding and not breathing normally" or "Not responding, but breathing normally" in the First Aid Knowledge Pack.

DO NOT

- Do not stay inside to find the cause first. CPSC says get outside to fresh air immediately. [CPSC]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- Feeling better outside does not tell you when it is safe to go back in. No source read says when it is.

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Fresh air needs nothing. Get outside. [CPSC]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- If someone is still inside, do not go back into a space with bad air if you cannot make it safe; this is from First Aid's Start here in any emergency. CDC and CPSC give no other step for someone who cannot be got out.

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- CPSC gives only the two steps above, fresh air and then emergency help. [CPSC] No source read covers what to do when no help can come, or how to keep people warm once they are outside. Staying in fresh air is our reading. Watch for the signs in Hypothermia and Frostbite.

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Fresh air. [CPSC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Only CPSC gives the two steps above. The CDC page does not say what to do when poisoning is suspected.

- No source read says when the home is safe again, or how to air it out.

- CDC and CPSC do not say how to get someone out of a space full of fumes. First Aid's Start here says not to go into a closed space with bad air if you cannot make it safe.

- No source read says whether to turn off the device on the way out. CPSC says to get outside immediately.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Medical treatment. Not in these sources.
