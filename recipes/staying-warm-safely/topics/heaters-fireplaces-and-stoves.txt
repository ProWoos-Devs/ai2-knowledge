Heaters, fireplaces and stoves

Search terms: space heater safety, electric heater, kerosene heater, paraffin heater, wood stove, wood burner, fireplace, how far from the heater, can I leave the heater on at night, chimney, fuel.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Using heaters, fireplaces and wood stoves safely when the main heating is off

Not for: Generators, grills and camping stoves, which must stay outside. See Never use these indoors.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- You will heat the home with a space heater, kerosene heater, fireplace or wood stove

WHAT TO DO

1. Follow the maker's instructions for the heater. [Storm]

2. Keep anything that can burn at least 1 meter from every heat source (USFA says 3 feet; we have rounded it up): fireplaces, wood stoves, radiators, space heaters and candles. That includes curtains, furniture and bedding. [USFA, Storm]

3. Turn space heaters off when you leave the room or go to sleep. [USFA]

4. Use fireplaces, wood stoves and other fuel-burning heaters only if they are vented to the outside and do not leak fumes into the room. [Storm, Prepare]

5. If you must use a kerosene (paraffin) heater, make sure it has proper ventilation. CDC gives no amount. [Storm]

6. Use only the fuel the heater was made for. Do not substitute another. [Storm]

7. For electric space heaters: choose one with an automatic shut-off switch and no glowing parts; keep it off furniture and away from water; avoid plugging it into an extension cord; keep the cord from tripping anyone, but not under rugs; stop using it if the cord is damaged or it sparks. [Storm]

8. Have heating equipment and chimneys cleaned and inspected every year by a professional; CDC says a qualified technician. A chimney blocked by debris can make carbon monoxide build up inside the home. [USFA, CO, Prepare]

9. If you heat with a fireplace, wood stove or kerosene heater, put a smoke alarm and a battery carbon monoxide alarm near the area being heated. See Carbon monoxide and smoke alarms. [Prepare]

DO NOT

- Do not cover a space heater. [Storm]

- Do not leave children alone near a space heater. [Storm]

- Do not burn paper in a fireplace. [Storm]

- Do not heat the home with an oven or a cooking stove. [USFA, CO, Storm]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If anyone gets a headache, dizziness, sickness or confusion while a fuel-burning heater runs, get everyone outside into fresh air at once, then get emergency help from outside. [CPSC] Then see If you think it is carbon monoxide.

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- With no vented fireplace or safe heater, rely on blankets, layers and keeping heat in. See Keeping a home warm. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- A fireplace that is up to code (meets the local building rules) with plenty of dry firewood, or a gas log fireplace (a gas fire made to look like logs), is one of the heat sources CDC lists for a power cut. It still has to be vented to the outside. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep a multipurpose dry-chemical fire extinguisher nearby [Prepare], and keep at least 1 meter around every heat source clear; USFA says 3 feet. [USFA]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A vent to the outside for anything that burns fuel. [Storm, Prepare]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- CDC tells readers to check with the local fire service whether kerosene heaters are legal where they live. No source read gives how much fresh air a kerosene heater needs.

- CDC's preparation page says all fuel-burning equipment should be vented to the outside. Its winter storm page says fireplaces, wood stoves and other combustion heaters must be properly vented to the outside, and also lists kerosene heaters as a heat source, saying they need proper ventilation, and says vent-less gas fireplaces need some ventilation. No source says how much. [Prepare, Storm]

- The meters are our own rounding up of 3 feet, which is 0.91 meters.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Building or installing a stove or chimney. The sources give only one point, for gas appliances: a horizontal vent pipe should rise slightly toward the outside so carbon monoxide does not leak from loose joints. [CO]
