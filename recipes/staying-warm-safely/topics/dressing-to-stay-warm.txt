Dressing to stay warm

Search terms: what to wear in the cold, layers, wool, cotton, hat, mittens or gloves, keep warm clothes, sweating, wet clothes, shivering, going outside in the cold.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Clothing and habits that keep the body warm indoors and on short trips outside

Not for: Warming someone who is already too cold. See Hypothermia.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The home is cold, or you must go outside in cold weather

WHAT TO DO

1. Wear several layers of loose-fitting clothing. [Storm]

2. Next to the skin, wear fabrics that hold body heat and do not soak up moisture. CDC names wool, silk or polypropylene as holding more heat than cotton. [Storm]

3. Over that, an insulating layer such as wool, goose down or fleece traps warm air close to the body. [Storm]

4. Outside, the top layer should be tightly woven, and preferably resist wind and water. [Storm]

5. Wear a hat, a scarf or knit mask over face and mouth, sleeves snug at the wrist, mittens rather than gloves because they are warmer, and a water-resistant coat and boots. [Storm]

6. Stay dry. Wet clothing chills the body quickly. [Storm]

7. Take off a layer when you feel too warm. Sweating makes the body lose more heat. [Storm]

8. Keep trips outside short. [Storm]

DO NOT

- Do not ignore shivering. CDC calls it an important first sign that the body is losing heat, and constant shivering means it is time to go inside. [Storm]

- Do not get petrol (gasoline) or alcohol on your skin in the cold. It makes the body lose much more heat. [Storm]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If someone shivers and becomes confused, clumsy, very tired or drowsy, they may have hypothermia, which is a medical emergency. Get them into a warm place and get medical help as soon as possible. [Hypo, Frost] See Hypothermia.

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Blankets, sleeping bags and coats worn indoors. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- When stranded in a car, CDC says to wrap the whole body, head included, in extra clothing, blankets or newspapers, and to huddle with other people for warmth. If you are stranded in a car outdoors in a winter storm and run the engine for heat, CDC says to run it about 10 minutes per hour, with one window slightly open, and to make sure snow is not blocking the exhaust pipe. Never run a car in a garage attached to a home. [Storm, CO] Do not eat snow; it lowers body temperature. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Heavy work in the cold strains the heart. Work slowly to avoid sweating, and follow a doctor's advice if you have heart disease or high blood pressure. [Storm]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Dry clothing. [Storm]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The sources give the clothing advice for going outdoors. Using the same layers indoors when the heating is off is our reading of it.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Travel in winter storms. Not covered here.
