Carbon monoxide

Search terms: carbon monoxide, CO, poison gas, fumes, invisible killer, gas with no smell, headache from heater, stove fumes, generator fumes, what is carbon monoxide.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Knowing what carbon monoxide is and where it comes from

Not for: What to do when someone may already be poisoned. See If you think it is carbon monoxide.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- You will burn any fuel indoors or near the home: wood, coal, charcoal, gas, oil, kerosene or petrol
- You want to know the signs

WHAT TO DO

1. Know what it is. Carbon monoxide is a gas with no color and no smell. It can kill without warning. [CO, CPSC]

2. Know where it comes from. It is in the fumes whenever fuel burns: cars and trucks, small engines, generators, stoves, lanterns, grills, fireplaces, gas cookers and furnaces (central-heating boilers that burn gas or oil). It can build up indoors and poison people and animals. [CO]

3. Know the signs: headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, sleepiness and confusion. They are often described as "flu-like". [CO, CPSC]

4. Know who is most at risk. Everyone is at risk. Babies, older people and people with heart disease, anemia or breathing problems are more likely to get sick from it. [CO]

5. Know the worst danger. Breathing a lot of it can make a person pass out or kill them. People who are asleep, drunk or under the influence of drugs can die before they have any signs. [CO]

6. Read Never use these indoors and Carbon monoxide and smoke alarms. [CO, CPSC]

DO NOT

- Do not rely on smell to find it. It has none. [CO, CPSC]

- Do not take flu-like signs lightly while something is burning fuel indoors. [CO]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If anyone has these signs while something burns fuel indoors or nearby, see If you think it is carbon monoxide. [CO, CPSC]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Alarm or no alarm, keep fuel-burning devices out of the home or vented to the outside. See Never use these indoors. [Prepare, CO]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Blankets, layers and closed-off rooms add warmth without any fumes. See Keeping a home warm. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- No source read gives a treatment for carbon monoxide poisoning at home beyond getting to fresh air, so keeping it out of the home is the main protection. See Never use these indoors. [CPSC, CO]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A working carbon monoxide alarm, where you can have one. [CO, CPSC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- CDC lists chest pain and upset stomach; CPSC lists nausea and sleepiness. Both lists are combined here.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Numbers of deaths in the United States, which the sources give for that country only.
