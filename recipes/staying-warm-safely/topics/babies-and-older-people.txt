Babies and older people in the cold

Search terms: baby cold at night, keep the baby warm, infant sleeping in cold room, sleep sack, grandmother cold, elderly neighbor, older people heating, who is most at risk from cold.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Keeping babies and older people warm when the heating is off

Not for: Someone who already shows signs of hypothermia. See Hypothermia.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- A baby or an older person is in a cold home

WHAT TO DO

Babies

1. A baby under one year old should never sleep in a cold room. Babies lose body heat more easily than adults. [Storm]

2. Dress the baby warmly, for example in footed pajamas, a one-piece wearable blanket or a sleep sack. [Storm]

3. Take pillows and other soft bedding out of the baby's sleeping place. They raise the risk of smothering and sudden infant death. [Storm]

4. Try to keep the home warm. If you cannot, arrange to stay somewhere else for a while. [Storm]

5. In an emergency, you can keep a baby warm with your own body heat. If you must sleep, take care not to roll onto or smother the baby, and keep pillows and soft bedding out of the baby's sleeping place. [Storm]

Older people

6. Older people often make less body heat. Check on older relatives and neighbors often to see that their homes are warm. [Storm]

7. People over 65 should keep an easy-to-read thermometer where they see it often, and check the home's temperature often in cold weather. The ability to feel a change in temperature drops with age. [Prepare, Storm]

DO NOT

- Do not put soft bedding in with a sleeping baby to keep it warm. [Storm]

- Do not leave children alone near a space heater. [Storm]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- A baby with bright red, cold skin and very low energy may have hypothermia, which is a medical emergency. Get medical help as soon as possible. [Hypo, Frost] See Hypothermia.

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- If the home cannot be kept warm, arrange to stay somewhere warmer. In an emergency your own body heat can keep a baby warm. If you must sleep, take care not to roll onto or smother the baby, and keep pillows and soft bedding out of the baby's sleeping place. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Footed pajamas, a one-piece wearable blanket or a sleep sack instead of loose bedding. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Older people with too little food, clothing or heating, and babies sleeping in cold bedrooms, are among those who most often get hypothermia. CDC also lists people who drink alcohol or use illicit drugs, and people who stay outdoors for long periods. [Hypo]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A warm room for a baby to sleep in. In an emergency, CDC's fallback is your own body heat, taking care not to roll onto the baby. [Storm]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- No source read gives a room temperature that is warm enough for a baby.

- CDC's winter storm page gives only the sleep points above. It gives no other way to set up a baby's sleeping place in a cold home.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Feeding babies when the power is off. See the Food Without Power Knowledge Pack.
