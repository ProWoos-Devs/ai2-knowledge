Hypothermia

Search terms: hypothermia, too cold, body temperature low, shivering and confused, slurred speech, drowsy in the cold, warm someone up, baby cold and red, person very cold, cold and wet.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Someone whose body is getting too cold

Not for: Frozen skin on fingers, toes, nose or ears. See Frostbite.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- An adult shows shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech or drowsiness in the cold [Hypo]
- A baby has bright red, cold skin and very low energy [Hypo]

WHAT TO DO

1. Hypothermia is a medical emergency. Get medical help as soon as possible. [Hypo, Frost] If you have a thermometer, take the person's temperature. Below 35 °C (95 °F), get medical help at once. [Hypo]

2. Whether or not you have a thermometer, if the person shows these signs and you cannot get medical help right away, warm them: [Hypo]

3. Get them into a warm room or shelter. [Hypo]

4. Take off any wet clothing. [Hypo]

5. Warm the center of the body: chest, neck, head and groin. Use skin-to-skin contact under loose, dry layers of blankets, clothing, towels or sheets, or an electric blanket if there is power. [Hypo]

6. Warm drinks can help. Never give a drink to an unconscious person, and never give alcohol. [Hypo]

7. Once their temperature has risen, keep them dry and wrap the body, head and neck included, in a warm blanket. [Hypo]

8. Get them proper medical care as soon as possible. [Hypo]

DO NOT

- Do not give alcohol. [Hypo]

- Do not give drinks to an unconscious person. [Hypo]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- A person with severe hypothermia may be unconscious and seem to have no pulse or breathing. Handle them gently and get emergency help at once. [Hypo] If the person does not respond and is not breathing normally, give CPR, as in "Not responding and not breathing normally" (adult or teenager, child, or baby) in the First Aid Knowledge Pack. CDC says to give CPR even if the person appears dead, keep warming them during CPR, and continue until they respond or medical help takes over. Some who appear dead can be revived. [Hypo]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Skin-to-skin contact under dry layers needs nothing but people and covers. [Hypo]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Dry clothing, towels or sheets instead of blankets. [Hypo]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep warming the center of the body and keep the person dry. The brain is affected, so a person with hypothermia may not know it is happening and cannot help themselves. [Hypo]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Getting out of the cold and out of wet clothing. [Hypo]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Hypothermia can happen even in cool weather above 4 °C (40 °F) if a person gets chilled by rain, sweat or cold water. [Hypo]

- The temperatures are given in Fahrenheit by CDC; 35 °C and 4 °C are our own conversions.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Rescue from water and CPR after drowning. See Drowning in the First Aid Knowledge Pack. To warm someone taken out of cold water, use the steps above. [Hypo]
