Frostbite

Search terms: frostbite, frozen fingers, numb toes, white skin from cold, waxy skin, frost nip, ears frozen, warm frozen hands, red painful skin from cold.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Skin and flesh damaged by freezing, usually nose, ears, cheeks, chin, fingers and toes

Not for: A person whose whole body is too cold. See Hypothermia, which comes first.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- Skin has gone red or painful in the cold, which may be the beginning of frostbite [Frost]
- Skin is white or grayish-yellow, feels unusually firm or waxy, or is numb [Frost]
- Frostbite can cause permanent damage, and people with poor circulation, or not dressed for the cold, are more likely to get it [Frost]

WHAT TO DO

1. At the first redness or pain, get out of the cold or cover the exposed skin. [Frost]

2. Check for signs of hypothermia too: in adults shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech or drowsiness; in babies bright red, cold skin and very low energy. [Hypo] Hypothermia is more serious and needs emergency medical care. See Hypothermia. [Frost]

3. Seek medical care for frostbite. [Frost]

4. If there are no signs of hypothermia and no medical care is available, get the person into a warm room as soon as possible. [Frost]

5. Put the frostbitten parts in warm, not hot, water. The water should feel comfortable to unaffected skin. [Frost]

6. If there is no warm water, warm the part with body heat, for example frostbitten fingers in an armpit. [Frost]

DO NOT

- Do not walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless you must. It adds to the damage. [Frost]

- Do not rub the area with snow or massage it at all. [Frost]

- Do not warm it with a heating pad, heat lamp, stove, fireplace or radiator. Numb skin burns easily. [Frost]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the person shows signs of hypothermia, treat that first. See Hypothermia. [Frost]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- If there are no signs of hypothermia, medical care is not available and there is no warm water, body heat such as an armpit can warm frostbitten fingers. [Frost]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- If there are no signs of hypothermia and medical care is not available, warm water that feels comfortable on unaffected skin. [Frost]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- The steps above are what CDC gives for when medical care is not available. CDC still says frostbite should be checked by a health worker when one can be reached. [Frost]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Gentle warming. Rubbing and direct heat both add damage. [Frost]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- A person with frostbite may not notice it, because the frozen parts are numb. Others may need to point it out. [Frost]

- No source read says what to do if the frostbitten part may freeze again before reaching a warm place.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Blisters and wound care after frostbite. Not in these sources.
