Start here: staying warm safely when the heating is off

Search terms: no heating, power cut in winter, cold house, how to keep warm, blackout cold, heater, stove, carbon monoxide, where do I start, freezing at home.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Households keeping warm at home when the power, gas or heating has stopped

Not for: Being caught outdoors or in a car for long. The sources cover that only briefly.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The heating has stopped and the house is getting cold
- You are about to use a stove, heater, fireplace, generator or candles for heat or light
- You do not know which topic to read first

WHAT TO DO

1. Keep the heat you have. Close off rooms you do not need, stuff towels in the gaps under doors, cover windows at night and open outside doors as little as possible. See Keeping a home warm. [Storm]

2. Dress in loose layers, stay dry and use blankets and sleeping bags. See Dressing to stay warm. [Storm]

3. Before using anything that burns fuel, read Never use these indoors and Carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide has no color and no smell, and it can kill people in their sleep. [CO]

4. Keep anything that can burn at least 1 meter (3 feet) from every heat source, and turn heaters off when leaving the room or going to sleep. See Heaters, fireplaces and stoves. [USFA]

5. Check on babies and older people often. They lose heat faster. See Babies and older people in the cold. [Storm]

6. Learn the signs of hypothermia and frostbite. See those topics. [Hypo, Frost]

DO NOT

- Do not heat the home with a gas oven or a cooking stove. [CO, USFA, Storm]

- Do not run a generator, a charcoal grill or a camp stove inside, even with windows open. [CO, Storm, CPSC]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If someone gets a headache, dizziness, sickness or confusion while a fuel-burning heater, stove or generator is running, see If you think it is carbon monoxide. [CO, CPSC]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Blankets, sleeping bags and warm coats are the heat source that needs no fuel. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Flashlights and battery lanterns instead of candles. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep the heat in the rooms you use, and close off the rooms you do not need. [Storm]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Fresh air for anything that burns fuel indoors. A fireplace, wood stove or heater that burns fuel must be vented to the outside. [Storm, Prepare]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The sources are written for the United States and give distances in feet. The meters are our own rounding.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Food when the power is off. See the Food Without Power Knowledge Pack.
