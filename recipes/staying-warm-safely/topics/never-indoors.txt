Never use these indoors

Search terms: can I use a barbecue inside, charcoal indoors, camping stove inside, gas oven for heat, heat the house with the cooker, generator in the garage, car running in garage, chemical heater, brazier, unsafe heating.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: What must never be burned or run inside the home

Not for: Heaters and stoves made and vented for indoor use. See Heaters, fireplaces and stoves.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- You are thinking of using something that burns fuel to heat, cook or light the home during a power cut

WHAT TO DO

Keep all of these out of the home, the basement and the garage:

1. Generators, even with doors and windows open. Run them outside only. See Generators. [CO, CPSC, Storm]

2. Charcoal of any kind, whether it looks red, gray, black or white. Burning charcoal always gives off carbon monoxide. [CO]

3. Gas or charcoal grills and barbecues. [Storm]

4. Portable gas camping stoves. [CO, Storm]

5. Portable flameless chemical heaters. [CO]

Also:

6. Never run a car or truck in a garage attached to the home, even with the door open. In a detached garage, always open the door first. [CO]

7. Never heat the home with an oven of any kind, or by turning on the cooking stove. [USFA, CO, Storm]

8. Never cook or burn anything on a stove or in a fireplace that is not vented to the outside. [CO]

9. Do not keep petrol (gasoline) indoors, where its fumes could catch fire. [Storm]

DO NOT

- Do not use generators, gas or charcoal grills, camping stoves or similar devices near windows either. CDC says the fumes are deadly. [Storm]

- The sources say open doors and windows do not make a generator or a car in an attached garage safe. [CO, CPSC]


IF THINGS CHANGE

- If someone feels ill while one of these is running, get everyone outside into fresh air at once, then get emergency help from outside. [CPSC] Then see If you think it is carbon monoxide.

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Blankets, sleeping bags and warm coats give warmth with no fumes. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- A fireplace or wood stove that is properly vented to the outside and does not leak fumes into the room, or a heater made for indoor use. See Heaters, fireplaces and stoves. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Being cold is dangerous, but none of these is a safe answer to it. See Keeping a home warm and Dressing to stay warm. [CO, Storm]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Keeping these outside. [CO, CPSC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- CDC does not say whether an ordinary gas cooker counts as "not vented". It says never to heat the home with a gas oven or by turning on the stove. [CO, Storm]

- Never burn charcoal indoors, in a brazier or in anything else. [CO] For a brazier burning wood or other fuel, the sources give no separate rule. CDC's line covers burning anything on a stove or in a fireplace that is not vented; that it also covers such a brazier is our reading.

- Never use a portable gas camping stove indoors, even for a few minutes. CDC gives no exception for short use. [CO]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Cooking with no power. Not in these sources beyond what is above.
