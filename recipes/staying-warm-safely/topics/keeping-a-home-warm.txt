Keeping a home warm

Search terms: keep the heat in, cold house no heating, close off rooms, drafts, towels under doors, cover the windows, one warm room, insulate, frozen pipes, candles for light, flashlight.

Written from CDC, U.S. Fire Administration and CPSC sources. Not reviewed by a fire safety or health specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Holding on to the heat in the home when the heating is off

Not for: Heaters and stoves. See Heaters, fireplaces and stoves.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The heating has stopped and the home is getting colder

WHAT TO DO

1. Close off rooms you do not need. [Storm]

2. Stuff towels or rags into the gaps under doors. [Storm]

3. Close curtains, or cover windows with blankets, at night. [Storm]

4. Do not open outside doors and windows when you do not need to. [Storm]

5. Have blankets, sleeping bags and warm coats ready. CDC lists them as a heat source for a power cut. [Storm]

6. Eat well-balanced meals; they help you stay warmer. Drink warm, sweet drinks or broth. [Storm]

7. For light, use flashlights or battery lanterns rather than candles. If you use candles, never leave them burning alone. [Storm]

8. To stop pipes freezing, let taps drip slightly and open the cupboard doors under sinks so warmer air reaches the pipes. [Storm]

DO NOT

- Do not drink alcohol or caffeinated drinks to keep warm. They make the body lose heat faster. [Storm]

- Do not seal a room completely if you use a gas heater with no vent, such as a vent-less gas fireplace. Those need some fresh air. [Storm]

- Do not thaw frozen pipes with a flame. [Storm]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If you cannot keep the home warm and a baby is there, CDC says to make arrangements to stay somewhere warmer. See Babies and older people in the cold. [Storm]

- If the pipes burst or stay frozen, see the Water and Sanitation Knowledge Pack for other water. [Storm]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Blankets, coats, towels and curtains need no fuel. [Storm]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Plastic over the windows, put on from the inside, is one of CDC's ways to keep heat in. [Prepare]

- If there is no other water, snow can be melted. Boiling it kills most germs but does not remove chemicals. [Storm]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Keep the warmth in the rooms you use and close off the rest, and check the people most at risk often. See Babies and older people in the cold. [Storm]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Staying dry. Wet clothing chills the body quickly. [Storm]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- No source read gives a temperature for a home that is too cold. CDC asks people over 65 to keep an easy-to-read thermometer where they see it often. [Prepare]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Insulating walls and attics, and sealing doors and windows before winter. Mentioned by CDC as preparation, not something to do during a power cut. [Prepare]

Source: CDC, Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning Basics, 2026 (CO); CDC, Safety Guidelines: During and After a Winter Storm, 2026 (Storm); CDC, Preparing for a Winter Storm, 2026 (Prepare); CDC, Preventing Hypothermia, 2026 (Hypo); CDC, Preventing Frostbite, 2026 (Frost); U.S. Fire Administration, Heating Fire Safety, 2023 (USFA); U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, Carbon Monoxide (CPSC).
