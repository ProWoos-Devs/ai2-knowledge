Dates and times in PHP

date('Y-m-d H:i') prints the current date and time, for example 2026-11-17 18:30. In the format, Y is the four-digit year, m and d the month and day with leading zeros, H the hour from 00 to 23, i the minutes and s the seconds.

Times come out in the time zone PHP is told to use. AI-2's php.ini leaves date.timezone unset, so PHP uses UTC and times can be hours off from the local clock. Set it for one script with date_default_timezone_set('Africa/Nairobi'); or for everything by putting date.timezone = Africa/Nairobi in /etc/php/php.ini. The names are Region/City, and DateTimeZone::listIdentifiers() in PHP lists them all.

For anything beyond printing the date, use DateTimeImmutable. It knows leap years and summer time, and modify never changes the original.

$start = new DateTimeImmutable('2026-01-15');
$next = $start->modify('+1 month');
$days = $start->diff(new DateTimeImmutable('2026-12-25'))->days;

Adding months has a trap. From 31 January, +1 month gives 3 March, or 2 March in a leap year, because February has no 31st and the extra days roll over. When the end of the month is what you mean, use modify('last day of next month').

To read a date in a known format, DateTimeImmutable::createFromFormat('!d/m/Y', '17/11/2026') returns the date at midnight, or false if the text does not have that shape. The ! sets the missing time to zero, otherwise the current time of day is filled in. A date that does not exist, such as 31/02/2026, is not refused but rolled over to 3 March, so to check a date a visitor typed, also compare $d->format('d/m/Y') with the text.

Store times in the database as UTC, in a DATETIME column or as a Unix timestamp from time(), and convert to the visitor's time zone only when showing them. That avoids hours lost or doubled when the clocks change.

Do not add 86400 seconds to move one day. On the day summer time starts or ends, a day is 23 or 25 hours long. Use modify('+1 day').
