A BODY-WIDE MESSAGE SYSTEM

The nervous system helps the body receive information, process it and respond. It includes the brain, spinal cord and nerves. These work together in sensing, thinking, learning, remembering and controlling body activities. A nervous system is not only for thoughts you notice; it also helps regulate activities that continue without a deliberate decision each time. [S10, Introduction to the Nervous System; S10B, Autonomic Nervous System; web.]

The brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system. The brain lies inside the skull. The spinal cord connects with the brain and lies protected within the backbone. Nerves branching from the brain and spinal cord connect this central system with other parts of the body. The spinal cord is nervous tissue, not the bone surrounding it. [S10A, opening explanation and Spinal Cord; S10B, opening explanation; web.]

RECEIVE, PROCESS, RESPOND

Sensory receptors detect changes inside or outside the body. These include changes in light, sound, temperature and internal conditions. Sensory input is information carried in electrical signals. The nervous system processes that input, helping create sensations, thoughts and memories. A response can involve signals to muscles or glands, which are organs that release substances. [S10, sensory, integrative and motor functions; web.]

For example, imagine seeing a familiar shape and deciding to draw it. Information about light reaches the nervous system; the brain helps recognize what you see; messages to muscles help move your hand. This is a simplified example of receiving information, processing it and responding. It does not mean each body activity always follows one slow, conscious chain. [S10, sensory, integrative and motor functions; S20, How the Eyes Work; web.]

NERVES AND AUTOMATIC WORK

A nerve contains bundles of nerve fibers. Sensory fibers carry information toward the central nervous system, while motor fibers carry messages toward muscles and other responding structures. Some nerves contain both kinds of fiber. Different parts of the system communicate, rather than every nerve doing exactly the same job. [S10B, Structure of a Nerve; web.]

Some nervous-system activity helps control the heart, digestive organs and other internal parts. These automatic controls work continually without requiring a person to think "do that now" before every action. Deliberate movement and automatic regulation are both parts of how the body works. [S10B, opening explanation and Autonomic Nervous System; web.]

REFLEXES

A reflex is a response that does not need conscious thought before it begins. Some reflexes are organized through the spinal cord. A withdrawal reflex can move an affected body part before the person becomes aware of the pain. The brain is still informed, but waiting for a considered decision is not required for the movement to start. This describes a body process, not an activity to test with pain. [S10A, Spinal Cord; web.]
