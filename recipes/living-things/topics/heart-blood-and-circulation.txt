A PUMP AND A NETWORK

The heart is a muscle-containing organ in your chest, between your lungs, about the size of your fist. It pumps blood around the body. The heart and blood vessels form the circulatory system. Blood carries oxygen and nutrients, useful substances from food, to body parts. It carries carbon dioxide, a waste gas, toward the lungs to be breathed out. [S11, The Heart; S11A, Supplying oxygen to the heart's muscle; S12B, opening explanation; S45, Blood; S13, Why is digestion important?; web.]

The heart's electrical system controls the rate and rhythm of the heartbeat, the repeated pumping action. Blood contains red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. Red blood cells carry oxygen using a protein called hemoglobin. The lungs transfer oxygen from inhaled air into blood; the heart moves that blood onward. [S11, The Heart; S8, marrow explanation; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

FOUR CHAMBERS AND VALVES

The heart has a right atrium and right ventricle, and a left atrium and left ventricle. These are its four chambers, or internal spaces. Blood enters an atrium and is pumped into a ventricle. The ventricle pumps it out of the heart. Valves open and close to keep blood moving forward. [S11A, Heart valves and Adding oxygen to blood; web.]

FOLLOW ONE CIRCUIT

Blood returning from the body, low in oxygen, enters the right atrium. It moves into the right ventricle, which pumps it toward the lungs. In the lungs, blood gains oxygen and releases carbon dioxide. Blood with more oxygen then returns to the left atrium and moves into the left ventricle. [S11A, Adding oxygen to blood; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

The left ventricle pumps blood out through a large artery called the aorta toward the rest of the body. Blood delivers oxygen to cells, then returns through the circulation toward the heart and lungs. This is a repeating journey, not a one-way delivery that ends after a single trip. [S11A, Adding oxygen to blood; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

ARTERIES AND VEINS

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins bring it toward the heart. The names describe direction, not oxygen content. The pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs; the pulmonary veins bring oxygen-rich blood back. Here pulmonary means relating to the lungs. Capillaries are tiny blood vessels connected to the network of arteries and veins. In body tissues, materials such as oxygen pass between blood and cells through capillaries. [S11A, opening explanation and Adding oxygen to blood; S12B, Air fills your lung's air sacs; S47, opening explanation, Capillaries and Veins; web.]

The heart muscle also needs oxygen and nutrients. Its own coronary arteries supply them. On paper, trace body -> right heart -> lungs -> left heart -> body, then add arrows showing direction. The arrows show blood flow, rather than airflow. [S11A, Supplying oxygen to the heart's muscle and Adding oxygen to blood; web.]
