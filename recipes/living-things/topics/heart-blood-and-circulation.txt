A PUMP AND A NETWORK

The heart is an organ that pumps blood through the body. Blood travels in vessels, including arteries, veins and tiny capillaries. Together the heart and blood vessels make up the circulatory system. Blood carries oxygen and nutrients that body parts need. It also carries carbon dioxide, a waste gas, toward the lungs so it can leave the body in breathed-out air. [S11, The Heart; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

The heart is made of living tissue, including muscle. Its activity moves blood rather than making the oxygen itself. The lungs transfer oxygen from inhaled air into the blood. Heart and lungs therefore have different jobs that fit together. Neither organ alone describes the whole journey of oxygen to the body's cells. [S11, The Heart; S12, The Lungs; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

FOUR CHAMBERS AND VALVES

The heart has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles. An atrium receives blood, and a ventricle pumps blood onward. The right and left sides handle different parts of the circulation. Valves open and close to keep blood moving in the required direction, rather than flowing backward between steps. These are parts of one coordinated pump. [S11A, Heart valves and Adding oxygen to blood; web.]

FOLLOW ONE CIRCUIT

Blood returning from the body, low in oxygen, enters the right atrium. It moves into the right ventricle, which pumps it toward the lungs. In the lungs, blood gains oxygen and releases carbon dioxide. Blood with more oxygen then returns to the left atrium and moves into the left ventricle. [S11A, Adding oxygen to blood; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

The left ventricle pumps blood out through a large artery called the aorta toward the rest of the body. Blood delivers oxygen to cells, then returns through the circulation toward the heart and lungs. This is a repeating journey, not a one-way delivery that ends after a single trip. [S11A, Adding oxygen to blood; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

ARTERIES AND VEINS

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins bring it toward the heart. These names describe direction, not whether the blood has much oxygen. The pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood toward the lungs, and the pulmonary veins bring oxygen-rich blood back. Those examples prevent the mistaken rule that every artery carries oxygen-rich blood. [S11A, opening explanation and Adding oxygen to blood; web.]

The heart muscle also needs oxygen and nutrients. Its own coronary arteries supply them. On paper, trace body -> right heart -> lungs -> left heart -> body, then add arrows showing direction. This simplified model helps explain the journey without replacing the separate roles of chambers, valves and blood vessels. [S11A, Supplying oxygen to the heart's muscle and Adding oxygen to blood; web.]
