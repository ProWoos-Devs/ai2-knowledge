SUPPORT AND PROTECTION

The skeleton is the framework of bones inside the body. It supports soft body parts and works with muscles to produce movement. Bones in the legs support the body when standing. The skull surrounds the brain; the backbone protects the spinal cord; the rib cage helps protect the heart and lungs. These structures provide protection while also being parts of a living body. [S8, Introduction to the Skeletal System; web.]

Bones are living structures, not lifeless sticks. They contain active tissues, use nutrients and oxygen, and need a blood supply. Bone can change its structure in response to the forces placed on it. This ongoing activity is one reason a skeleton should be understood as living tissue rather than as a fixed model made of dry white pieces. [S8, living-bone explanation; web.]

WHERE BONES MEET

A joint is a place where bones meet. Some joints allow a great deal of movement, some only a little, and some are immovable. The joined bones of the skull provide examples of joints that do not move freely. A joint is therefore not automatically a hinge that can swing open and shut. Its structure affects the movement it permits. [S8A, Articulations, Synarthroses and Amphiarthroses; web.]

In freely movable joints, a tissue called cartilage covers the ends of the bones. A capsule encloses the joint, ligaments help hold the bones together, and fluid inside lubricates their movement. These soft tissues matter as well as the bones themselves. A skeleton drawing that shows only bone leaves out parts needed to understand a working joint. [S8A, Diarthroses; web.]

HOW MUSCLES MOVE BONES

Skeletal muscles consist of bundles of specialized muscle cells. When they contract, they produce force. Tendons attach muscles to bones, commonly on opposite sides of a joint. As a muscle contracts, its attachment can move a bone. Nerve signals help start the contraction, and blood vessels supply the muscle. Movement depends on several body systems cooperating. [S9A, Structure of Skeletal Muscle; web.]

Muscles also help maintain posture, meaning how the body is held. They make adjustments when a person sits or stands, and help stabilize joints. Visible movement is not their only job. Muscles can be working even when the body appears fairly still. [S9, posture and joint stability; web.]

MORE THAN THE MUSCLES YOU NOTICE

Skeletal muscle helps move the skeleton. Other muscle types include cardiac muscle in the heart and smooth muscle in organs such as the digestive tract. These internal muscles work without a separate conscious decision for every contraction. Bones have other jobs too: red marrow within certain bones produces blood cells. The body's support, movement and internal work are closely connected. [S9B, Muscle Types; S8, marrow explanation; web.]
