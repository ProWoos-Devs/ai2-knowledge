SMALL UNITS THAT DO WORK

Cells are the smallest units that carry out the activities of life. An organism can consist of one cell or many cells working together. Your body contains many kinds of cells with different shapes and jobs. Almost all are microscopic, meaning too small to see separately with your unaided eyes. Plants, like animals, are made of many cells. Bacteria are examples of living things made of just one cell. [S7, Cell Structure & Function; S7A, Cell Structure; S39, Definition; web.]

The body begins as a single cell and develops into a body with many cells. Growth involves producing new cells and cells increasing in size. Cell division also helps replace worn-out cells and repair damaged parts of the body. [S7, Cell Structure & Function; S7A, Cytoplasm; S7B, Cell division; web.]

BOUNDARY, CONTENTS AND INSTRUCTIONS

A cell membrane is the boundary around a cell. It separates inside from outside and controls the movement of materials across that boundary. A cell needs exchanges with its surroundings; it is not a sealed bag that keeps everything forever. Some movements across membranes require the cell to use energy. [S7A, Cell membrane; S7B, Movement of substances across the cell membrane; web.]

Inside a typical human cell is cytoplasm, a gel-like material where many chemical reactions happen. Small structures called organelles do particular jobs. The nucleus contains DNA, the genetic material that provides instructions for making proteins. Proteins help form body structures and carry out many activities. This description is a useful model of a cell, rather than a claim that every cell has exactly the same parts and shape. [S7A, Nucleus and Nucleolus, Cytoplasm and Cytoplasmic organelles; S7B, DNA replication and protein synthesis; web.]

HOW NEW CELLS FORM

In a common kind of body-cell division, called mitosis, one parent cell produces two new cells. Each new cell receives the same set of genetic instructions as the parent, rather than half a set. The cytoplasm divides too. Mitosis helps the body grow and replace cells; it is different from the special division that makes egg and sperm cells. [S7B, Cell division; web.]

DIFFERENT JOBS IN ONE BODY

Muscle cells are specialized for contraction, the action that produces muscle movement. Nerve cells help carry messages. Bone contains living cells too; it is not just a collection of lifeless sticks. These examples show why the shape and structure of a cell matter. A muscle cell and a nerve cell cannot be treated as interchangeable pieces simply because both are cells. [S9, Introduction to the Muscular System; S10B, Structure of a Nerve; S24, opening explanation; S8, Introduction to the Skeletal System; S7B, Cell Function; web.]
