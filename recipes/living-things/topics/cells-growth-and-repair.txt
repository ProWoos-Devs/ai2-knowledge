SMALL UNITS THAT DO WORK

Cells are the smallest units that carry out the activities of life. An organism can consist of one cell or many cells working together. Your body contains many kinds of cells with different shapes and jobs. Almost all are microscopic, meaning too small to see separately with your unaided eyes. Their small size does not make their work unimportant. [S7, Cell Structure & Function; S7A, Cell Structure; web.]

The body begins as a single fertilized cell and develops into a body with many cells. Growth involves producing new cells and cells increasing in size. Cell division also helps replace cells and repair tissues. These are ordinary body processes, not something a person has to remember to start each time they grow. [S7, Cell Structure & Function; S7A, Cytoplasm; S7B, Cell division; web.]

BOUNDARY, CONTENTS AND INSTRUCTIONS

A cell membrane is the boundary around a cell. It separates inside from outside and controls the movement of materials across that boundary. A cell needs exchanges with its surroundings; it is not a sealed bag that keeps everything forever. Some movements across membranes require the cell to use energy. [S7A, Cell membrane; S7B, Movement of substances across the cell membrane; web.]

Inside a typical human cell is cytoplasm, a gel-like material where many chemical reactions happen. Small structures called organelles do particular jobs. The nucleus contains DNA, the genetic material that provides instructions for making proteins. Proteins help form body structures and carry out many activities. This description is a useful model of a cell, rather than a claim that every cell has exactly the same parts and shape. [S7A, Nucleus and Nucleolus, Cytoplasm and Cytoplasmic organelles; S7B, DNA replication and protein synthesis; web.]

HOW NEW CELLS FORM

In a common kind of body-cell division, called mitosis, one parent cell produces two new cells. Nuclear material is divided, and the cytoplasm divides too. New cells need the genetic information needed for their work. This is different from simply stretching one cell until it becomes an entire adult body. The body grows through organized growth and division. [S7B, Cell division; web.]

DIFFERENT JOBS IN ONE BODY

Muscle cells are specialized for contraction, the action that produces muscle movement. Nerve cells help carry messages. Bone contains living cells too; it is not just a collection of lifeless sticks. These examples show why the shape and structure of a cell matter. A muscle cell and a nerve cell cannot be treated as interchangeable pieces simply because both are cells. [S9, Introduction to the Muscular System; S10, Introduction to the Nervous System; S8, Introduction to the Skeletal System; S7B, Cell Function; web.]
