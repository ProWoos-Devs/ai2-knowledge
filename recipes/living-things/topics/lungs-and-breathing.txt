Lungs and breathing

Search terms: lungs, breathing, breathe in, breathe out, oxygen, carbon dioxide, diaphragm, windpipe, trachea, air sacs, alveoli, gas exchange, respiratory system.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

AIR AND BLOOD HAVE DIFFERENT PATHS

The two lungs are organs in the chest where gases pass between air and blood. Breathing in is inhaling; breathing out is exhaling. Oxygen moves from inhaled air into the blood, while carbon dioxide moves from the blood into air that will be breathed out. This exchange helps supply body cells and remove a waste gas. The air and blood do not simply pour together into one tube. [S12, The Lungs; S12A, Gas exchange; web.]

The respiratory system includes the lungs, airways and muscles used for breathing. The circulatory system, made up of the heart and blood vessels, brings blood to and from the lungs. Understanding breathing therefore involves both the movement of air and the movement of blood. One system supports the other. [S12B, opening explanation; S12, The Lungs; web.]

THE AIR'S JOURNEY

Air enters through the nose or mouth and passes down the windpipe, also called the trachea. Two main bronchial tubes branch toward the lungs. Inside the lungs, these divide into smaller tubes called bronchioles. They lead to clusters of tiny air sacs called alveoli. This branching route brings air close to many small blood vessels. [S12B, Airways and Air comes into your body; web.]

Air sacs are surrounded by capillaries, tiny blood vessels. Oxygen passes through the thin air-sac walls into the blood. Red blood cells pick up oxygen using a protein called hemoglobin. At the same time, carbon dioxide passes from blood into the sacs. It can then leave through the airways when the person exhales. [S12A, Gas exchange; S12B, Air fills your lung's air sacs; web.]

WHAT MAKES AIR MOVE?

The diaphragm is a breathing muscle. During inhaling it contracts and moves downward, increasing space inside the chest. Muscles between the ribs also help enlarge that space. The lungs expand and air moves in. During ordinary breathing out, these muscles relax, the chest space becomes smaller and air flows out. [S12A, Breathing in and Breathing out; web.]

During physical activity, additional muscle effort can help push air out more rapidly. The brain controls how fast or slowly you breathe by sensing the body's need for oxygen and its need to remove carbon dioxide. These explanations describe how ordinary breathing works, rather than an instruction to hold or change your breathing. [S12A, Breathing out; S12, The Lungs; web.]

WHERE THE GASES GO

Oxygen-rich blood returns from the lungs to the left side of the heart, which pumps it to the body. Cells use oxygen to help release usable energy. In doing so, they produce carbon dioxide, a waste gas. This passes into blood and travels back toward the lungs. On paper, keep two paths distinct: air through the airways, and blood through vessels. Mark where gases cross between them at the air sacs. [S12A, Gas exchange; web.]
