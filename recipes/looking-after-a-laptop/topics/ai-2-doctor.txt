Checking a laptop's health with ai-2 doctor

Search terms: ai-2 doctor, check the laptop, laptop health, is the disk OK, battery health, temperature check, WARN, FAIL, what does ai-2 doctor mean.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

RUNNING IT

Open a terminal (Terminal Emulator in the Applications menu), type ai-2 doctor and press Enter. It checks AI-2 itself and three things about the computer: each built-in disk, the battery and the processor's temperature. Each line starts with ok, WARN or FAIL, or with nothing when it only gives information. At the end it says whether there were warnings.

THE DISK

The disk keeps its own record of damage, which AI-2 reads every time the computer starts. "sudo ai-2 doctor" reads it at that moment, as administrator.
- "no damage recorded" is good.
- A WARN line saying that sectors were moved away from damaged places, or that there were read errors, ends with "The disk still works, but this is how a failing disk starts: keep a copy of what matters on another disk".
- A FAIL line ends with "Copy your files to another disk now; this one can stop working at any time".
The topic "A disk that is failing" says what to do.

A separate Disk line gives the free space in megabytes (MB). It is marked WARN under 2000 MB and FAIL under 500 MB; the Linux Essentials topic on a full disk says how to make room.

THE BATTERY

The Battery line says how much of its original charge the battery still holds, marked WARN under half. The topic "The battery" explains it.

THE TEMPERATURE

The Temperature line gives the processor's temperature now and the point at which this computer slows itself down or switches itself off. Close to that point it is marked WARN and says to check that the fan turns and the air vents are clean. When AI-2 is already running the processor slower to keep it cool, the line says so. The topic "Heat, the fan and the air vents" explains it.

WHEN TO RUN IT

When a laptop seems wrong, after it has been stored or carried far, and regularly on every laptop you look after.
