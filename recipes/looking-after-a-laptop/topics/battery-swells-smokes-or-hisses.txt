A battery that swells, smokes or hisses

Search terms: swollen battery, battery bulging, battery puffed up, laptop does not close, touchpad lifting, battery smoking, battery hissing, popping sound, battery fire, burning smell, punctured battery, battery leaking, battery very hot.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

IF IT SMOKES, POPS, HISSES OR BURNS

Leave it where it is. Get everyone outside the building and away from the smoke, and call the fire service from a safe place outside. Do not go back in for the laptop. The U.S. Fire Administration's handout on batteries after flooding advises moving to a safe distance and notifying the local fire department when a battery gives off smoke or gas, pops or hisses, and the same holds for any battery. Its home fire escape advice is to get outside, stay at the meeting place and call the fire department.

If no fire service can come, keep everyone outside and away. Do not decide it is safe to touch because it has been quiet or cool for a while, because the U.S. Fire Administration warns that a lithium-ion battery fire can start again after it seems to be out.

SIGNS TO STOP USING A BATTERY

The U.S. Fire Administration says to stop using a lithium-ion battery that is smoking, hissing, punctured or swollen, or that gives off an odor, changes color, gets too hot, changes shape, leaks or makes odd noises.

HOW A SWOLLEN BATTERY SHOWS

A battery inside a laptop that swells pushes on whatever is next to it. The base may bulge or rock on the table, the touchpad or the keyboard may lift, the lid may not close flat, or the case may split at the seams. A battery that comes out may look puffed up and no longer fit.

IF IT IS SWOLLEN, PUNCTURED OR DAMAGED, BUT NOT SMOKING

1. Stop using it and do not charge it again.
2. Disconnect the charger only if you can do it at the wall without handling the damaged laptop.
3. Do not press on it, bend it, puncture it or try to take the battery out.
4. Keep people away from it, and tell everyone it is not to be used.
5. The EPA advises asking the maker of the battery or the laptop for how to handle a damaged one. Ask them, or the local authorities, before moving it.

If it starts to smoke, pop or hiss, go back to the first section.
