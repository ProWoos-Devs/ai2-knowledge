Charging safely

Search terms: charge the laptop, charger, which charger, power strip, extension cord, multiplug adapter, charging many laptops, charger gets hot, damaged cord, charging overnight.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

THE RIGHT CHARGER

The U.S. Fire Administration advises always using the charger provided by the manufacturer for the device being charged, or an original or certified replacement charger and cable. A charger that fits the plug is not always the right one.

STRAIGHT INTO A WALL OUTLET

The U.S. Fire Administration advises plugging a charging device directly into an outlet, not into an extension cord or a multiplug adapter, and not overloading outlets, because multiplug adapters or power strips on one outlet can overheat and become a fire hazard. When in doubt, its flyer on lithium-ion batteries advises having a licensed electrician test the wiring. Its page "Appliance and Electrical Fire Safety" adds: push plugs fully into sockets, never force a plug with three pins into an outlet made for two, and have an outlet replaced if plugs do not fit snugly.

WHILE IT CHARGES

The U.S. Fire Administration advises charging on a solid, flat surface, not on a bed or anything soft, not in direct sunlight, and not leaving devices charging unattended when possible. In a shared classroom, that means charging while someone is in the room and stopping before the room is left empty.

CORDS AND CHARGERS

- Replace worn, old or damaged extension cords right away.
- Do not put cords where they can be damaged or pinched, such as under a carpet or a rug.
- If an appliance has a cracked, damaged or loose cord, the U.S. Fire Administration says to replace the appliance; for a laptop, that means replacing the charger.

These three points come from the U.S. Fire Administration's "Appliance and Electrical Fire Safety".

A HOT CHARGER

A charger is often warm while it works. Stop using a charger that smells of burning, crackles or shows melting. Unplug it only if the wall plug and outlet are safe to reach and handle. If the outlet is damaged, scorched or the plug fits loosely, leave that outlet unused and have it checked. If there is smoke or fire, get everyone outside and call the fire service from a safe place. Replace a damaged charger with an original or certified replacement. This section is ordinary care built on the U.S. Fire Administration advice above.

MANY LAPTOPS

Charging a whole classroom from one power strip goes against this advice. Charge a few laptops at a time from wall outlets, while someone is there. The topic "Looking after many laptops" has a routine for it.
