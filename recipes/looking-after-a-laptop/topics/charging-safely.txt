Charging safely

Search terms: charge the laptop, charger, which charger, power strip, extension cord, multiplug adapter, charging many laptops, charger gets hot, damaged cord, charging overnight.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

THE RIGHT CHARGER

The U.S. Fire Administration advises always using the charger provided by the manufacturer for the device being charged, or an original or certified replacement charger and cable. A charger that fits the plug is not always the right one.

STRAIGHT INTO A WALL OUTLET

The U.S. Fire Administration advises plugging a charging device directly into an outlet, not into an extension cord or a multiplug adapter, and not overloading outlets: multiplug adapters or power strips on one outlet can overheat and become a fire hazard. When in doubt, its flyer on lithium-ion batteries advises having a licensed electrician test the wiring. Its page "Appliance and Electrical Fire Safety" adds: push plugs fully into sockets, never force a plug with three pins into an outlet made for two, and have an outlet replaced if plugs do not fit snugly.

WHILE IT CHARGES

The U.S. Fire Administration advises charging on a solid, flat surface, not on a bed or anything soft, not in direct sunlight, and not leaving devices charging unattended when possible. Charge when someone is in the room, not overnight in an empty classroom.

CORDS AND CHARGERS

- Replace worn, old or damaged extension cords right away.
- Do not put cords where they can be damaged or pinched, such as under a carpet or a rug.
- If an appliance has a cracked, damaged or loose cord, the U.S. Fire Administration says to replace the appliance; for a laptop, that means replacing the charger.

These three points come from the U.S. Fire Administration's "Appliance and Electrical Fire Safety".

A HOT CHARGER

This part is ordinary care, not from a source. A charger is often warm while it works. If it is too hot to hold, smells of burning, has marks of melting or makes a crackling sound, unplug it at the wall if you can do it safely, stop using it and replace it.

MANY LAPTOPS

Charging a whole classroom from one power strip goes against this advice. Charge a few laptops at a time from wall outlets, while someone is there. The topic "Looking after many laptops" has a routine for it.
