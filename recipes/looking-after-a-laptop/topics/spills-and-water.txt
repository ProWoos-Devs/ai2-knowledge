Spills and water

Search terms: spilled water on the laptop, spilled tea, juice on the keyboard, laptop got wet, rain, flood, wet laptop, laptop fell in water, keys sticky after a spill.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

AT ONCE, AFTER A SPILL

1. Disconnect the laptop from its charger. For battery-powered devices that may have been in a flood, the U.S. Fire Administration advises disconnecting them from chargers, and the same first step is right after a spill. If the charger, its cord or the wall outlet is wet, do not touch them. If you can reach the switch or the fuse box with dry hands and without standing in water, switch the power off there first; otherwise ask someone who knows electricity.
2. Turn the laptop off at once: hold the power button down until it goes off. If its battery comes out without tools, take it out.
3. Wipe the outside dry with a soft cloth, then open the lid and stand the laptop upside down like a tent, so that liquid can run out of the keyboard.

The steps after the first are ordinary care, not from a source.

LET IT DRY

Leave the laptop off, open, in a dry, airy place out of strong sun, for at least a few days. Do not turn it on or charge it while it may still be wet inside. A laptop that works after drying may still have sticky keys from a sweet drink; that is a nuisance, not a danger.

WATCH THE BATTERY

The U.S. Fire Administration warns that spills or flooding can make a battery fail even days later, even when the laptop looks fine. For the next days, charge it only while someone is in the room, and if the battery smokes, makes a popping or hissing sound, or swells, follow the topic "A battery that swells, smokes or hisses".

AFTER A FLOOD

For devices whose lithium-ion batteries have been in a flood, the U.S. Fire Administration advises telling the local fire department, disconnecting them from chargers, taking them out of buildings, and moving to a safe distance and notifying the fire department if a battery gives off smoke or gas, pops or hisses. It advises putting flood-damaged battery-powered products in a metal or other non-combustible tub, at least 6 feet (about 2 meters) away from debris and buildings, and never putting them in garbage or recycling bins or with storm debris. The topic "Old batteries and broken laptops" says how to get rid of one.
