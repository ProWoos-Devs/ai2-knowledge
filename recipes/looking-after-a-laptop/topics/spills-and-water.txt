Spills and water

Search terms: spilled water on the laptop, spilled tea, juice on the keyboard, laptop got wet, rain, flood, wet laptop, laptop fell in water, keys sticky after a spill.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

FIRST, LOOK FOR DANGER

If the laptop smokes, pops or hisses, leave it alone, get everyone outside and follow the topic "A battery that swells, smokes or hisses".

IF THE CHARGER, CORD OR OUTLET IS WET

Do not touch them. The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) advises turning off the power at the main breaker or fuse when electrical equipment has got wet, never while standing in water, and calling an electrician to do it if the main switch cannot be reached without entering water.

AFTER A SMALL SPILL

1. If the wall outlet and the plug are dry and safe to reach, unplug the charger at the wall.
2. If the power button is dry and safe to touch, turn the laptop off.
3. Keep it off and unplugged. Leave the battery and the case alone.

These steps are ordinary care, written from the sources above.

BEFORE USING IT AGAIN

The U.S. Fire Administration warns that spills or flooding can make a battery fail even days later, and a laptop that turns on is no proof that its battery is safe. The CDC says electrical equipment must be completely dry before it is used again. If liquid may have reached the battery, or you cannot tell how far it went, keep the laptop out of use and ask the maker or someone who repairs laptops before turning it on or charging it.

AFTER A FLOOD

The U.S. Fire Administration's handout on batteries after flooding advises:
- notifying the local fire department even if you only suspect that batteries were flooded;
- if a battery gives off smoke or gas, pops or hisses, leaving it alone, moving to a safe distance and notifying the fire department;
- disconnecting flooded devices from chargers and taking them out of buildings, when that is safe;
- putting flood-damaged battery-powered products in a metal or other non-combustible tub, at least 6 feet (about 2 meters) from debris and buildings, and never in garbage or recycling bins or with storm debris.

Keep a flooded laptop out of use and do not charge it.
