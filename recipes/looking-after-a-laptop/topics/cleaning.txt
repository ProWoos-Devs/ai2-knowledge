Cleaning dust, the keyboard and the screen

Search terms: clean a laptop, dust in the laptop, clean the keyboard, crumbs in the keyboard, clean the screen, dirty screen, clean the air vents, sticky keys.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

No agency publishes advice on cleaning a laptop, so this topic is ordinary care. It never asks you to open the laptop.

BEFORE YOU START

Save the work, shut the laptop down (Applications menu, Log Out, then Shut Down) and unplug the charger. Clean only a laptop that is off and cool.

THE AIR VENTS

The air vents are the slots or small holes where the fan blows air in or out, usually on a side, the back or underneath. Look at them in good light. Lift away the dust you can see with a soft, dry brush, working from the outside. Do not push anything into the vents.

THE KEYBOARD

With the laptop off and cool, open the lid only as far as it moves easily. Keep the base on the table and lift out loose crumbs and sand with a soft, dry brush. Leave stuck keys or grit trapped under keys for someone who repairs laptops. A key that sticks after a spill belongs to the topic "Spills and water".

THE SCREEN

Wipe the screen gently with a soft, dry, clean cloth. Do not press hard, because the screen is the most fragile part. Never pour or spray water or any cleaning liquid onto the screen or the keyboard.

THE OUTSIDE

Wipe the lid and the base with a soft, dry cloth. Dust and dirt on the outside matter less than dust in the air vents, which is what makes a laptop run hot.

HOW OFTEN

Where there is dust or sand in the air, look at the air vents often, for example every week. The topic "Looking after many laptops" has a simple routine for a school.
