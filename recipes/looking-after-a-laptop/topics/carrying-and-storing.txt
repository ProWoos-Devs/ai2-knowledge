Carrying and storing laptops

Search terms: carry a laptop, laptop bag, move laptops, store laptops, laptops for the holidays, keep laptops for a long time, stack laptops, lift a laptop by the screen.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

No agency publishes advice on carrying a laptop, so most of this topic is ordinary care.

CARRYING ONE

- Shut it down before you put it in a bag. A laptop that is still running in a closed bag cannot let its heat out.
- Close the lid, and carry it with both hands under the base, never by the screen.
- Use a bag with some padding if you have one, and do not put heavy or sharp things against the screen.
- Unplug the charger before moving it, so nobody trips on the cord and the plug is not pulled sideways.
- The U.S. Fire Administration advises not putting lithium-ion batteries in direct sunlight or keeping them in hot cars.
- Let a laptop brought in from the cold warm to room temperature. If there may be water drops on it or in it, keep it off and unplugged until it is dry.

MOVING MANY

Carry a few at a time. Do not stack laptops so high that the ones at the bottom are pressed, and never put anything heavy on a closed lid, because the screen is under it.

STORING THEM

For e-bikes, scooters and power tools, which use the same kind of battery as a laptop, the U.S. Fire Administration advises dry, ventilated spaces away from things that can burn and away from the building's exits. A dry shelf or cupboard that is not closed up tight, away from paper, cloth and fuel, is a good place for laptops.

AFTER A LONG TIME STORED

No agency source says how full to leave a battery for long storage. When laptops come back out after weeks or months, look at each one for damage before charging it. If a battery or a case has swollen, keep that laptop out of use and follow the topic "A battery that swells, smokes or hisses". Charge only the laptops that pass the check, as in "Charging safely", and check them with ai-2 doctor.
