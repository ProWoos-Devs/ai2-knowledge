Old batteries and broken laptops

Search terms: throw away a battery, dispose of a laptop, recycle a laptop, old battery, broken laptop, dead battery, where to take batteries, e-waste.

Written for AI-2, for the person who looks after the laptops. Not reviewed by a repair technician.

NOT IN THE TRASH

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) says that lithium-ion batteries, and devices that contain them, should not go in household garbage or recycling bins, because they can start fires. The U.S. Fire Administration says the same. The EPA adds that even used batteries can have enough energy to injure or start fires.

GETTING IT READY

These steps are only for batteries and laptops that are not damaged. A swollen, punctured, leaking or flooded battery is handled as the section on a damaged battery below says.

To prevent fires, the EPA advises covering a loose battery's terminals (the metal contacts) with non-conductive tape, such as electrical tape, or putting the battery in its own plastic bag. A laptop whose battery is inside stays whole and goes in its own bag.

WHERE TO TAKE IT

The EPA advises taking lithium-ion batteries to separate recycling or household hazardous waste collection points, sending laptops and other devices with these batteries to certified electronics recyclers, and sending batteries that come out easily to battery recyclers or shops that take them back. The U.S. Fire Administration advises asking the local authorities how used batteries are collected in your area.

A DAMAGED BATTERY

The EPA advises contacting the maker of the battery or the laptop for how to handle a damaged one. Keep it out of use, do not tape or bag it yourself, and ask the maker or the local waste authority. For a flooded one, follow the topic "Spills and water"; for one that smokes, pops or hisses, follow "A battery that swells, smokes or hisses".

WHERE THERE IS NO COLLECTION POINT

Neither the EPA nor the U.S. Fire Administration says what to do where no collection exists, so the following is ordinary care. Until an undamaged battery can be taken away, keep it taped or bagged, in a dry place, apart from other batteries, away from anything that can burn and from the building's exits, out of the reach of children, and ask the local authorities.

THE REST OF THE LAPTOP

A laptop that no longer works may still have a good charger. Reuse it only if it is undamaged and is the original or a certified replacement for the laptop that will use it, as the U.S. Fire Administration advises. The other parts are inside the case and are for someone who repairs laptops. Take the battery out only if it is made to be removed by the user, is not damaged and comes out normally; then tape or bag it as above.
