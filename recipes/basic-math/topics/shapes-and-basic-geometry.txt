FLAT (2D) SHAPES

Two-dimensional (2D) shapes are completely flat shapes that have only length and width, with no thickness. You can draw them on a flat piece of paper.
- Circle: A perfectly round curved shape where every point along the curved boundary is the exact same distance from the center. It has no straight sides and no corners.
- Triangle: A polygon (a flat closed shape with straight sides) with 3 straight sides and 3 corners (called vertices). Triangles can have three equal sides (equilateral), two equal sides (isosceles), or three different sides (scalene).
- Square: A 4-sided polygon where all 4 sides are exactly equal in length, and all 4 corners are square right angles (90 degrees).
- Rectangle: A 4-sided polygon with 4 square right angles, where opposite sides are equal in length.
- Pentagon: A polygon with 5 straight sides and 5 vertices.
- Hexagon: A polygon with 6 straight sides and 6 vertices, shaped like the cells in a honeybee comb.

SOLID (3D) SHAPES

Three-dimensional (3D) shapes are solid objects that have length, width and height. They take up physical space and you can hold them in your hands.
- Sphere: A perfectly round ball, like an orange or a soccer ball. It has 1 curved surface and 0 edges or vertices.
- Cube: A solid box where all 6 faces are identical flat squares. It has 6 flat faces, 12 straight edges and 8 sharp vertices (corners).
- Cylinder: A shape with 2 identical flat circular ends and 1 curved tube-like surface, shaped like an aluminum food can.
- Cone: A shape with 1 flat circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a single pointed vertex at the top, like an ice-cream cone.
- Pyramid: A solid with a flat polygon base and flat triangular sides that meet at a point at the top.

PARTS OF A 3D SHAPE: FACES, EDGES AND VERTICES

- Face: A flat or curved surface that forms the outer skin of the shape.
- Edge: The straight line where two flat faces meet.
- Vertex (plural: vertices): A sharp point or corner. On a shape with flat faces it is where three or more edges come together. The tip of a cone is also called a vertex.
A die for board games (which is a cube) has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
