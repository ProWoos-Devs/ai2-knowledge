Fractions and parts of a whole

Search terms: fractions, half, quarter, third, numerator, denominator, equal parts, fraction bar, equivalent fractions, comparing fractions, fraction, halves, thirds, fourths, one-fourth, top number, bottom number, pizza slices.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT A FRACTION IS

A fraction shows one or more equal parts of a whole object or group. When you cut a pizza into 4 identical slices and eat 1 slice, you have eaten one-quarter (written as 1/4) of the pizza. The parts must always be exactly equal in size for them to be true fractions.

NUMERATOR AND DENOMINATOR

A fraction is written with two numbers, one above the other, separated by a line called the fraction bar. On one line of text the bar is typed as a slash, as in 3/4:
- The denominator is the bottom number. It tells you the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into.
- The numerator is the top number. It tells you how many of those equal parts you have or are counting.
In the fraction 3/4 (three-quarters): 4 is the denominator (the whole has 4 equal parts) and 3 is the numerator (you have 3 of those parts).
A handy way to remember is: Denominator is Down at the bottom.

COMMON FRACTIONS

- One-half (1/2): The whole is divided into 2 equal parts, and you have 1.
- One-third (1/3): The whole is divided into 3 equal parts, and you have 1.
- One-quarter (1/4): The whole is divided into 4 equal parts, and you have 1.
- One whole: When the numerator and denominator are the same number (2/2, 3/3, 4/4), they equal 1 complete whole.

COMPARING FRACTIONS

When two fractions have the same denominator, the one with the larger numerator is bigger. For example, 3/5 of a pie is more than 2/5 of the same pie because 3 equal slices is more than 2 equal slices.
When two fractions have the same numerator (like 1/2 and 1/8), the one with the smaller denominator is larger. A pizza cut into only 2 pieces gives much bigger slices than a pizza cut into 8 pieces. So 1/2 is much bigger than 1/8.

EQUIVALENT FRACTIONS

Equivalent fractions are different fractions that name the exact same amount. Fold a sheet of paper in half, open it, and shade one of the 2 halves. Now 1/2 of the paper is shaded. If you fold it again across the middle, the shaded part now has 2 out of 4 equal boxes (2/4). The amount of paper shaded has not changed at all, so 1/2 is equivalent to 2/4.
You can find an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and the denominator by the same number (any number except zero). For 1/2, multiplying top and bottom by 2 gives 2/4; multiplying both by 3 gives 3/6.
