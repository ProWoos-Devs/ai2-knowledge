Telling time, clocks and the calendar

READING AN ANALOG CLOCK

An analog clock has a round face with the numbers 1 to 12 spaced evenly around it, and two main hands that turn clockwise:
- The short hand is the hour hand. Exactly on the hour it points at the hour number. After that it creeps slowly toward the next number, so the hour is the number it has just passed. It moves slowly, taking 12 hours to make one full rotation around the clock face.
- The long hand is the minute hand. It points to the minutes past the hour. It takes 60 minutes (1 hour) to go completely around the clock face once.
Each number on the clock face represents 5 minutes for the long minute hand: 1 means 5 minutes, 2 means 10 minutes, 3 means 15 minutes, 6 means 30 minutes, and 12 means 60 minutes (a new hour).

KEY CLOCK POSITIONS

- O'clock: When the long minute hand points straight up at 12. If the short hand points at 3, it is exactly 3:00 (three o'clock).
- Quarter past: When the minute hand points to 3 (15 minutes past the hour, one-quarter of the clock circle).
- Half past: When the minute hand points straight down at 6 (30 minutes past the hour, half of the circle).
- Quarter to: When the minute hand points to 9 (45 minutes past the hour, meaning 15 minutes remain before the next hour arrives).

DIGITAL CLOCKS: 12-HOUR AND 24-HOUR TIME

Digital clocks show time using digits separated by a colon, like 07:30.
- In 12-hour time, hours run from 1 to 12. We use AM for times between midnight and midday (morning), and PM for times between midday and midnight (afternoon and evening).
- In 24-hour time, the day starts at 00:00 (midnight) and counts up to 23:59. From 1:00 PM to 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour: 1:00 PM becomes 13:00, 5:30 PM becomes 17:30, and 8:00 PM becomes 20:00. The hour after noon stays as it is: 12:30 PM is 12:30. The hour after midnight starts with 00: 12:30 AM is 00:30.

UNITS OF TIME AND THE CALENDAR

Time is counted in fixed units:
- 60 seconds = 1 minute
- 60 minutes = 1 hour
- 24 hours = 1 day (the time from one noon to the next as the Earth spins)
- 7 days = 1 week
- About 52 weeks = 1 year (52 weeks and 1 day, or 2 days in a leap year)
- 12 months = 1 year (one complete orbit of the Earth around the Sun)
A regular year has 365 days. Almost every fourth year is a leap year with 366 days, adding an extra day (February 29) to keep the calendar aligned with Earth's orbit. The exception is a year ending in 00, which is a leap year only if it can be divided by 400: 2000 was a leap year, 2100 will not be.
Months have 30 or 31 days, except February which has 28 days (29 in leap years). The knuckle trick helps you remember: make a fist and count the months across your four knuckles and the valleys between them, starting with January on the first knuckle. July lands on the last knuckle. Then start again on the first knuckle with August. Months on a knuckle have 31 days (January, March, May, July, August, October, December) and months in a valley are shorter.
