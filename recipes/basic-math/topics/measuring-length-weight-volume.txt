THE METRIC SYSTEM AND PREFIXES

The metric system is the worldwide system of measurement based on tens, hundreds and thousands. Because it uses powers of ten, changing between metric units is done simply by multiplying or dividing by 10, 100 or 1,000.
Three prefixes are used very often in metric measurements:
- Milli- means one thousandth (1/1,000). A millimeter is one thousandth of a meter.
- Centi- means one hundredth (1/100). A centimeter is one hundredth of a meter.
- Kilo- means one thousand (1,000). A kilometer is one thousand meters.

MEASURING LENGTH AND DISTANCE

Length tells you how long, tall or wide an object is, or how far apart two places are.
- Millimeter (mm): Very tiny, about the thickness of a plastic bank card.
- Centimeter (cm): About the width of your pinky finger. 10 mm = 1 cm.
- Meter (m): About one long adult step or the width of a doorway. 100 cm = 1 m.
- Kilometer (km): Used for long road trips and distance between towns. 1,000 m = 1 km. A walk of 10 to 15 minutes is about 1 kilometer.

MEASURING MASS AND WEIGHT

Mass measures how much matter is inside an object. In everyday speech people call it weight.
- Milligram (mg): Far too light to feel in your hand. It is used for very small amounts, such as the contents of a medicine tablet.
- Gram (g): Light, about the weight of a small metal paperclip. 1,000 mg = 1 g.
- Kilogram (kg): Standard everyday weight. A large bag of rice, a thick textbook or a liter of water weighs about 1 kilogram. 1,000 g = 1 kg.
- Metric ton (t), also called a tonne: Used for very heavy things like cars and elephants. 1,000 kg = 1 metric ton.

MEASURING LIQUID VOLUME AND CAPACITY

Volume measures how much three-dimensional space something takes up. A container's capacity is how much it can hold when it is full. A 1-liter bottle that is half full has a capacity of 1 liter and holds 500 milliliters of liquid.
- Milliliter (mL): A very small amount. A teaspoon holds about 5 mL.
- Liter (L): A standard milk carton or large reusable water bottle. 1,000 mL = 1 L.
Water connects the metric system cleanly: 1 liter of pure freshwater has a volume of 1,000 milliliters and weighs almost exactly 1 kilogram.
