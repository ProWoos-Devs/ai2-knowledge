Start here: water and sanitation when services have stopped

Search terms: no water, no running water, tap stopped, water safe to drink, where do I start, disaster, no electricity, toilets not working, sanitation, hygiene.

Written from WHO sources. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: Households and small groups with no water supply or sewage service

Not for: Running a water system for a camp or a town. The WHO notes this topic draws on were written for that, and their planning steps are left out.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- Water stops coming from the tap, or you are no longer sure it is safe
- Toilets stop working
- You do not know which topic to read first

WHAT TO DO

1. Drinking water comes first. A few liters a day to drink matter more than water for washing or laundry, though people still need to wash to prevent skin disease. [TN 9.1]

2. Only water for drinking and preparing food needs treating. That is about 5 liters per person per day. [TN 5.1]

3. If the water is cloudy, clear it first by letting it stand and settle, or by straining it through cloth. See Clearing cloudy water. Settling alone does not make water safe. [TN 5.1, TN 5.3]

4. Then make it safe with one proven method: boiling, solar disinfection in clear PET plastic bottles, or chlorine. See the topic for each. [TN 5.2, SODIS p. 20]

5. Keep treated water safe: a clean, covered container, and hands and cups kept out of it. Treating water is wasted if it gets dirty again afterwards. See Storing water safely. [TN 5.3]

6. Keep feces away from water and food. Do not let anyone go to the toilet on the banks of a river, stream or pond used for water, or in fields of crops about to be eaten. Any well should be uphill and upstream of where people go. See Digging and siting a latrine. [TN 14.1, SAN p. 179]

7. Wash hands with soap after using the toilet, before preparing food, before eating, breastfeeding or feeding children, and after cleaning a child's feces. See Handwashing and hygiene. [TN 5.4, TN 10.1]

8. Cover water pots and keep down flies, which carry disease from feces to food. [TN 10.1]

DO NOT

- Do not assume clear water is safe. Settled or strained water still needs a proven method. [TN 5.1]

- Do not rely on boiling, sunlight or chlorine to remove chemical pollution. They do not. See What treatment cannot remove. [TN 5.1, SODIS p. 51]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If water smells of oil or petrol, or has a greasy film or shine, do not use it. [TN 15.1]

- If a well was flooded by the sea, see Cleaning a well. Salt is not a health risk at levels people find acceptable to drink, and slightly salty but disinfected well water can be better than fresh water from an unprotected source. [TN 15.2, TN 15.3]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Solar disinfection needs clear PET plastic bottles, such as used water bottles, clear water and sun, and does not work in continuous rain. [SODIS p. 8, SODIS p. 20, SODIS p. 22]

- Boiling needs fuel, which is costly. [TN 5.2]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Water for washing floors or watering crops does not need to be as clean as drinking water, so keep the best water for drinking. [TN 9.2]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Solar disinfection needs no fuel or chemicals, only PET bottles and sun. Boiling needs fuel, and chlorine needs bleach or tablets, which will run out. [TN 5.2, SODIS p. 8]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A source free of chemicals. No home method removes chemical pollution. [TN 5.1]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The sources assume that agencies, supplies or services still exist. None read covers what to do when bleach, tablets and other chemicals run out for good.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Diarrhea and dehydration care. A health topic, not covered by these sources.

- Disposal of dead bodies and of rubbish. In the WHO notes, not yet a topic.

Source: WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page); WHO Guidelines on Sanitation and Health, 2018 (SAN, PDF page); SODIS Manual, Eawag 2016 (PDF page).
