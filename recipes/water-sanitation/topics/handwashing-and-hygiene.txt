Handwashing and hygiene with little water or soap

Search terms: wash hands, handwashing, no soap, ash instead of soap, hygiene, diarrhea prevention, flies, germs, keep clean, how to wash hands properly.

Written from WHO sources. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: Everyone, to stop disease spreading from feces to food and water

Not for: Treating diarrhea. Not covered by these sources.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- Water and soap are scarce and you need to decide what hygiene matters most

WHAT TO DO

1. The habits that matter most: using and keeping latrines clean, getting rid of feces safely, washing hands, keeping drinking water safe, and keeping down flies and mosquitoes. [TN 10.1]

2. Wash hands with soap and water after going to the toilet, before preparing food, before eating, breastfeeding or feeding children, and after cleaning a child's feces. [TN 5.4]

3. Wash for at least 20 seconds. [TN 10.2]

4. How: wet your hands; cover all of them with soap; rub palm to palm; rub each palm over the back of the other hand; rub palm to palm with fingers laced; rub the backs of the fingers against the other palm; rub each thumb inside the other hand; rub the fingertips in a circle on the other palm; rinse well; let your hands dry completely before touching anything. Pay attention to under the nails. [TN 10.2]

5. Wash your hands with soap before handling drinking water. [TN 5.3]

6. Keep water pots covered. [TN 10.1]

7. People still need water to wash themselves, to prevent skin disease, after drinking water is covered. [TN 9.1]

DO NOT

- Do not assume everyone knows how to wash hands properly. Show them. [TN 5.3]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- Where people gather, put handwashing with soap near every latrine. [TN 13.3]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- No soap: WHO's chlorine tablet picture shows washing hands with water and soap or ash. Limits: The notes give no method for ash and do not compare it with soap. [TN 5.3]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Water for washing does not need to be as clean as drinking water. [TN 9.2]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- People change habits more readily for reasons such as pride and respect from neighbors than for health alone. Positive messages work better than frightening ones. [TN 10.2]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Handwashing at the times in step 2. [TN 5.4, TN 10.1]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- No source read gives a way to wash hands with very little water, or says how much water handwashing needs at home.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Running hygiene campaigns: radio, posters, drama, trained facilitators. In the WHO note, written for agencies.

- Menstrual hygiene. The WHO notes ask for a place to wash and dry menstrual cloths, which Digging and siting a latrine includes. No other household guidance found. [TN 13.3]

Source: WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page).
