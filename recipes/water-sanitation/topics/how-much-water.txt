How much water a person needs

Search terms: how much water, liters per person, water per day, ration water, survive on little water, water for animals, livestock water, water for garden.

Written from WHO sources. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: Planning water for a household or group

Not for: Medical needs of a sick person. Not covered.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- Water is scarce and you need to decide how much to collect, store or share

WHAT TO DO

1. Plan per person per day. The amount needed changes with climate, health and fitness. [TN 9.1]

2. Survival minimum, per person per day: 2.5 to 3 liters for drinking and food, 2 to 6 liters for basic hygiene, 3 to 6 liters for basic cooking. In all, 7.5 to 15 liters. [TN 9.2]

3. Aim higher when you can. Sphere calls 15 liters per person per day established practice, and never a maximum. In the worst phase of a drought, 7.5 liters may do for a short time. Research shows about 20 liters per person per day is the minimum for basic health and hygiene. [Sphere p. 132, TN 9.2]

4. Use water in this order of importance: drinking, cooking, washing yourself, washing clothes, cleaning the home, growing food. [TN 9.1]

5. Drinking water must be the cleanest. Water for cleaning floors, and even more so for watering crops, can be of lower quality. People can take drinking water from one source and wash clothes in a stream. [TN 9.2]

6. Of the total, only water for drinking and preparing food needs treating, about 5 liters per person per day. [TN 5.1]

7. Choose a toilet that uses little water. A flush toilet can use up to 7 liters per use. A pit latrine or a simple pour-flush toilet uses much less. [TN 9.2]

8. Animals and gardens, per day: cattle, horses and mules 20 to 30 liters each; goats, sheep and pigs 10 to 20 liters each; chickens 10 to 20 liters per 100 birds; vegetable gardens 3 to 6 liters per square meter. [TN 9.3]

DO NOT

- Do not put other uses before drinking. A few liters a day to drink matter more than water for washing or laundry. [TN 9.1]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If collecting water takes more than 30 minutes, people collect less. [TN 9.2]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Containers: for a household of 5, two containers of 10 to 20 liters for collecting and one of 20 liters for storing. [TN 9.2]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Wash and do laundry at the water source, if it is safe to do so, to avoid carrying water home. [TN 9.2]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Start by covering survival needs, then increase the amount step by step as more water can be found. [TN 9.3]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Drinking water. Nothing replaces it. [TN 9.1]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The figures are adapted from the Sphere standards for disaster relief, and WHO says to check them against local conditions. [TN 9.1]

- The WHO note suggests narrow-necked, covered containers. Another WHO note says wide-necked containers with a tight lid are best because they are easier to clean. See Storing water safely. [TN 9.2, TN 5.3]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Water for health centers, schools and places of worship. In the WHO note, but written for agencies.

Source: WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page); The Sphere Handbook, 4th edition, 2018 (Sphere, PDF page).
