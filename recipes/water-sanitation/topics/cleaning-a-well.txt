Cleaning a well, including after seawater flooding

Search terms: clean a well, flooded well, dirty well, well after flood, well after tsunami, salty well, seawater in well, disinfect a well, chlorinate a well.

Written from WHO sources. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: A hand-dug, open well that was damaged, flooded or polluted

Not for: Boreholes with pumps, or piped systems. In the WHO notes, not covered here.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- A well was flooded, filled with debris, or may be polluted

WHAT TO DO

1. Do not let anyone use the well while it is being cleaned. Its water will have a strong concentration of chlorine that tastes and smells bad and could be dangerous. [TN 1.4]

2. If the well was flooded by the sea: do steps 3 to 6 and 8 now, without chlorine, removing the water once and slowly as described in AFTER SEAWATER FLOODING. Then stop and wait for the salt to go down. Do steps 7 and 9 to 13 only after that. [TN 15.1, TN 15.2, TN 15.3]

3. If it smells of oil or petrol, or has a greasy film or shine on the water, do not use it at all. [TN 15.1]

4. If the walls or apron around it are cracked or undermined, the well should be abandoned, replaced or rebuilt. [TN 15.1]

5. Look for nearby sources of pollution: latrines, ponds, surface water and animal pens. Remove debris and polluted pools near the well. [TN 1.1, TN 15.1]

6. Remove floating debris with a sieve or bucket. Remove polluted water, sludge and debris with buckets or a pump. [TN 1.2, TN 15.1]

7. Clean the well lining with a brush and chlorine solution, from outside the well with a long-handled brush. For cleaning, make the solution with twice the powder given in step 11. [TN 1.2]

8. Put a 15 cm layer of gravel on the bottom. Seal the top of the well with clay. Build a drainage apron and a wall around the top to keep out surface water, insects and rodents, and cover the well. [TN 1.2]

9. Let the water rise back to its normal level, and check how cloudy it is. WHO chlorinates a well only when its water is very clear, under 5 turbidity units, and offers the coin test (see Clearing cloudy water) as its field estimate. If the coin disappears before the water is 50 cm deep, do not chlorinate: remove all the water again and let it refill. If the coin is still visible at 50 cm, the water is under about 10 units, as clear as the coin test can show; the sources do not say whether to go on in that case. WHO also checks pH with paper strips and, below pH 6, adds hydrated lime before chlorinating. [TN 1.2, TN 1.3, TN 1.4]

10. Work out the volume of water in the well, in cubic meters: 3.142 x diameter x diameter x depth of water, divided by 4, all in meters. [TN 1.2]

11. Disinfect, from outside the well, because the powder gives off chlorine gas, a serious danger: dissolve about 300 g of strong chlorine powder (high-strength calcium hypochlorite) in a 20-liter bucket of clear well water. For each cubic meter of water in the well, add 10 liters (half the bucket) of this solution. Stir the well with a long pole and leave it for at least 30 minutes. [TN 1.2, TN 1.4]

12. After that, remove all the water from the well. Do not let this water flow into streams or wetlands. Near the coast, pump slowly, not so fast that the well runs dry, so seawater is not drawn in. [TN 1.4, TN 15.1]

13. When it has refilled, wait another 30 minutes. WHO then measures the chlorine left in the water. Above 0.5 mg per liter, it removes all the water again and repeats the process. Below 0.5 mg per liter, WHO says the well is likely back to its condition before the disaster, which does not mean its water is safe to drink. Without a test kit, the sources give no other way to judge this. [TN 1.4]

14. Even after this, keep drinking water from the well safe at home: collect, handle and store it carefully, and treat it at home if you can. Disinfecting the well leaves no lasting protection. [TN 1.3]

AFTER SEAWATER FLOODING

- Salt is not a health risk at levels people find acceptable to drink. Slightly salty but disinfected well water can be better than fresh water from an unprotected source. [TN 15.2, TN 15.3]

- Do not deepen the well, and do not dig new deep wells near the coast, to reach fresh water below. [TN 15.3]

- Do not pump the well out again and again to lower the salt. Pumping more than the well holds slows the natural cleaning, and hard pumping can make the well salty. Remove the water once, slowly, taking care not to pump so fast that the well empties. Send the water to the sea or a river, downstream of other wells. [TN 15.1, TN 15.2, TN 15.3]

- After that, the water may be cloudy for up to a day. It can then be used for washing and cleaning, but not for drinking. [TN 15.2]

- Leave the well to clean itself naturally. This can take as long as one to two years, depending on rain and ground. Find other water for drinking meanwhile. [TN 15.2]

- Rain helps. Channel rainwater into hollows nearby so it soaks into the ground. Some malaria mosquitoes prefer to breed in salty standing water, so do not assume standing brackish water is free of them. [TN 15.3]

- When the community judges the water no longer too salty to drink, disinfect it with steps 9 to 13, pumping slowly when you empty it. [TN 15.1, TN 15.2, TN 1.4]

DO NOT

- Do not climb into a well being cleaned with chlorine. Chlorine gives off a dangerous gas. Entering needs full protective clothing, breathing equipment and a strong air flow. [TN 1.2]

- Avoid chlorinating a well repeatedly. It can pollute the groundwater and cause skin rashes in people who bathe in it. [TN 15.2]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the water stays cloudy after cleaning, the gravel at the bottom may have failed or surface water may be getting in. These cannot be fixed at once. Do not chlorinate the well while it is cloudy. WHO says it is probably safe to let people use it, because the water should be at least as good as before the disaster. Treat drinking water at home. [TN 1.2, TN 1.3]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Floating debris can be removed with a sieve or bucket, and water with buckets. WHO's drawing shows the apron as a concrete slab. [TN 1.2, TN 15.1]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Without strong chlorine powder, WHO also names liquid bleach and bleaching powder, but gives the dose only for strong chlorine powder. [TN 1.3]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Without a chlorine test kit, you cannot check the chlorine left in the well water the way WHO describes, and no source gives another method. [TN 1.4]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Time, for a salty well. WHO calls natural flushing by rain and fresh water soaking into the ground the best and quickest remedy. [TN 15.2, TN 15.3]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- WHO's well cleaning is meant to bring the well back to its condition before the disaster. A low chlorine reading afterwards does not mean the water is safe to drink. [TN 1.1, TN 1.4]

- WHO checks the well with a chlorine test kit after cleaning. Its clearness limit, 5 units, is below what its own coin test can show. [TN 1.2, TN 1.3, TN 1.4]

- WHO's general well note says to empty the well after the chlorine and to repeat the process if too much chlorine remains. Its seawater note says to avoid repeated chlorination and not to pump so fast that the well runs dry. For a coastal well this topic follows the seawater note's slow pumping. [TN 1.4, TN 15.1, TN 15.2]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Measuring pH and chlorine with test strips and kits. Needs equipment.

- Repairing pumps and boreholes. In the WHO notes, written for technicians.

Source: WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page).
