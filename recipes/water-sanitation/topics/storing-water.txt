Storing water safely

Search terms: store water, water container, keep water clean, bucket with lid, water storage, how to keep drinking water safe, jerrycan.

Written from WHO sources and the SODIS manual. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: Keeping treated water safe until it is drunk

Not for: Treating water. See Boiling water, Solar disinfection and Disinfecting with bleach or chlorine.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- You have treated water and need to keep it for hours or days

WHAT TO DO

1. Use a clean container with a cover, and keep it in a cool, dark place. [TN 5.3]

2. A wide-necked container such as a bucket with a tight-fitting lid is best, because it is easy to clean between uses. [TN 5.3]

3. Fit a tap to the container if you can, so water can be poured straight into a cup or bowl without hands or cups going into the container. [TN 5.3]

4. Wash your hands with soap before handling drinking water. [TN 5.3]

5. Keep water treated by solar disinfection in its bottles until it is drunk. [SODIS p. 8]

6. Keep boiled water covered while it cools and while it is stored. [Boil]

DO NOT

- Do not dip hands or cups into stored drinking water. [TN 5.3]

- Do not leave drinking water uncovered. [TN 5.3, TN 10.1]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If stored water has been touched by hands or dirty cups, or left uncovered, it may no longer be safe. Treating water is wasted if it gets dirty again afterwards. [TN 5.3]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Covered pots work. WHO's hygiene note shows covered water pots as a barrier against disease. [TN 10.1]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Without a tap, pour from the container rather than dipping into it. [TN 5.3]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Clean storage containers between uses. Wide-necked containers make this easier. [TN 5.3]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A cover. Uncovered water gets dirty again. [TN 5.3]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- One WHO note recommends wide-necked containers with lids because they are easy to clean. Another suggests narrow necks and covers for collecting and storing. Both agree on a cover. [TN 5.3, TN 9.2]

- No source read says how long treated water stays safe in storage.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Cleaning large tanks and water trucks. In the WHO notes, written for agencies.

Source: WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page); SODIS Manual, Eawag 2016 (PDF page); WHO Boil Water technical brief, 2015 (Boil).
