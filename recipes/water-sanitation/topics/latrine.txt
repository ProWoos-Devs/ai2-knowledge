Digging and siting a latrine

Search terms: latrine, toilet, pit latrine, dig a toilet, where to go to the toilet, no toilet, toilet not working, trench latrine, how far from water, sewage.

Written from WHO sources. Not reviewed by a water or health specialist.

For: Households and small groups with no working toilet

Not for: Sewers, septic tanks and camp-scale sanitation. Left to the WHO sources.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- Toilets no longer work and people need somewhere safe to go

WHAT TO DO

1. Choose the place first. Keep it away from water and food: never on the banks of a river, stream or pond used for water, and never in fields of crops about to be eaten. [TN 14.1]

2. Put it downhill of any well or spring used for drinking, and at least 30 meters away from it. Sphere gives 30 meters where the soil cannot be tested, more in cracked rock and limestone, less in fine soils. If there is no room for 30 meters, WHO's rule of thumb is at least 15 meters. [SAN p. 17, SAN p. 179, Sphere p. 140]

3. Keep the bottom of the pit at least 1.5 meters above the groundwater, where groundwater is used for drinking. [SAN p. 17, SAN p. 179, Sphere p. 140]

4. Put it at least 6 meters from where people live, and less than 50 meters' walk away. [TN 13.3]

5. In the first days, a simple family latrine is enough: a hole about 0.3 by 0.5 meters and 1 meter deep, with the dug soil piled beside it for covering, a screen for privacy and a place to wash hands. [TN 14.2]

6. For several people, a shallow trench: about 20 to 30 cm wide, 1.5 meters deep and 4 meters long, with a screen around it. People squat across it. Dig some narrower for small children and the elderly. Lay a plank along each side if the ground is soft. [TN 14.2]

7. Cover feces with soil after each use, and make sure all of it is covered at least once a day. [TN 14.2]

8. In a deep trench latrine, a daily layer of about 10 cm of soil reduces smell and stops flies breeding, and a ditch around it keeps surface water out. WHO's deep trench is 0.8 to 0.9 meters wide, 6 meters long and at least 2 meters deep, under a floor. Line the top 0.5 meters of the walls with plastic sheeting to stop the sides collapsing. [TN 14.2]

9. Close a trench by filling it with soil when the contents reach 30 cm from the surface, and dig a new one. [TN 14.2]

10. Trench latrines are temporary. For a longer stay, a pit latrine: WHO's example is at least 2 meters deep and 1 meter wide, preferably round. [TN 14.4, SAN p. 179]

11. Put handwashing near the latrine, and provide something for cleaning after use. [TN 13.3]

12. For privacy and safety: a door that locks from inside, and light at night where needed. Make room for washing and drying menstrual cloths. [TN 13.3]

DO NOT

- Do not let people go to the toilet near water sources or in food crops. [TN 14.1]

- Do not site a latrine uphill of a well. [SAN p. 179, TN 14.1]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- Rocky ground or high groundwater: build the pit up above the ground with wood, bamboo or stone, banked with soil. In practice only 1 to 1.5 meters high. [TN 14.4]

- Flooded area, or people on the move: use strong, watertight bags that seal. Collect them regularly and bury them. [TN 14.4]

- In a flood, and only if the water is flowing, a latrine built over the water is a last option. Think of anyone using the water or fields downstream. [TN 14.4]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- A spade and a screen of cloth or plastic sheeting are all a family latrine needs. [TN 14.2]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- If no latrine can be dug yet, set aside a screened area as far as possible from water. Use the strip farthest from the homes first, so nobody walks over used ground, and a new strip each day. Shallow trenches in the strips, covered with soil after use, are better. Limits: WHO says these areas are hard to manage and short-lived. Replace them with latrines as soon as you can. [TN 14.1, TN 14.2]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Trench latrines need daily covering and new trenches as old ones fill. Someone must look after them, or people stop using them. [TN 14.3]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Distance between feces and drinking water. [TN 14.1, SAN p. 17]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Distance from a water source: WHO's sanitation guidelines give at least 15 meters as a rule of thumb. The Sphere standards give at least 30 meters when the soil cannot be tested. This topic gives 30 meters where there is room and 15 meters as WHO's minimum. [SAN p. 17, Sphere p. 140]

- No source read gives a way to find how deep the groundwater is without a well nearby.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Borehole latrines, chemical toilets and portable toilet blocks. Need equipment.

- Emptying full pits and using their contents. Covered in the WHO sanitation guidelines, not yet a topic.

Source: WHO Guidelines on Sanitation and Health, 2018 (SAN, PDF page); WHO/WEDC Technical Notes on Drinking-water, Sanitation and Hygiene in Emergencies, 2nd edition, 2013 (TN, note and page); The Sphere Handbook, 4th edition, 2018 (Sphere, PDF page).
