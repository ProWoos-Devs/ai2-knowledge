Looking after the computer

Search terms: look after the computer, take care of a laptop, spilled water on the laptop, drinks near the computer, carry a laptop, the laptop is hot, keep it clean, dust, charger, battery, cable, protect the screen, the computer fell.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHY IT MATTERS

A computer can work for many years when it is looked after, and a school or a family may have only one. Most damage comes from a few things that are easy to avoid: water, falls, heat, dust and pulled cables.

FOOD AND DRINKS

Keep food and drinks away from the computer, on another table if you can. Water, juice or tea spilled on a keyboard can stop the computer working for good, and crumbs get under the keys. Wash and dry your hands before you use it.

If something is spilled on it, do not touch it yourself, and tell a grown-up at once. The grown-up should turn it off and unplug the charger.

CARRYING IT

Save your work and shut the computer down before you move it, or at least close the lid. Carry a laptop closed, with two hands, holding the bottom part, never by the screen. Do not put heavy books on top of it, and keep it away from the edge of a table where it can be knocked off.

KEEPING IT COOL

A computer makes heat when it works, and it has small holes, called air holes, to let the heat out. Use it on a hard, flat table, not on a bed, a pillow or a blanket, which block the holes. Keep it out of strong sunlight and away from a fire or a cooking stove. If it becomes very hot, smells of burning or makes smoke, stop using it and tell a grown-up at once.

KEEPING IT CLEAN

Dust and sand get into the keys and the air holes. When it is turned off, keep the computer covered or in a bag. A grown-up can clean the screen with a soft, dry cloth after turning the computer off. Never pour water or sprays onto it.

THE CHARGER AND CABLES

Do not pull a plug out by its cable, hold the plug itself. Do not bend cables tightly, step on them or leave them where someone can trip over them and pull the computer down. Only a grown-up should plug a charger into a wall socket. Always use a charger made for this computer. If you don't have one, consult with an adult first.

THE SCREEN

Open and close the lid gently, holding the middle of its top edge. Do not press on the screen, write on it, or close the lid on a pencil or an earphone left on the keyboard.

IF IT IS DAMAGED

If the computer falls, gets wet or stops working, tell a grown-up and do not try to open it. A grown-up can find someone who repairs computers.
