Learning to type

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHY LEARN TO TYPE

At first everyone types with one finger, looking for each letter. That works, but it is slow. Typing with both hands, using all your fingers, lets you write as fast as you think, and later you hardly need to look at the keys at all. It takes practice, a little every day, for weeks. Speed comes by itself. Start slowly and correctly.

GETTING READY

Put the computer on a table, with the keyboard straight in front of you. Rest your fingers lightly on the keys, curved a little, as if holding a small ball.

THE HOME ROW

The home row is the middle row of letters, where your fingers rest and come back to after each key. On an English keyboard:

- Left hand: little finger on A, then S, D, and the first finger (the one next to your thumb) on F.
- Right hand: first finger on J, then K, L, and the little finger on the key with the semicolon.
- Both thumbs rest on the Space bar.

Feel the F and J keys. Most keyboards have a small bump on each, so your first fingers can find home without looking.

ONE FINGER FOR EACH KEY

Each finger looks after the keys above and below its home key. The first fingers have the most work, because each also covers the keys next to it, toward the middle of the keyboard. After pressing a key, the finger goes back home.

HOW TO PRACTICE

1. Begin with the home row only. Type "asdf jkl" and short words made from those letters, like "lad", "sad", "fall", "ask".
2. Add one new key at a time, and practice words that use it.
3. Look at the screen, not at your hands. If you must look, look, then try again without looking.
4. Be correct first. A slow, correct line is better than a fast line full of mistakes.
5. Practice for ten or fifteen minutes, then stop and do something else. Short practice every day usually helps more than one long practice.

PRACTICE IDEAS

Copy a few lines from a book you like. Type a short story about your day, or a list of animals from A to Z. Type the same sentence three times and see whether the third is easier. Many computers have typing games, and a grown-up can help you find one.

When writing in a program, save your work as you go, so your practice is not lost.
