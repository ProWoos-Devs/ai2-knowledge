Passwords and staying safe

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT A PASSWORD IS FOR

A password is a secret word or sentence that proves it is really you. It keeps your files and your accounts yours. Anyone who knows your password can open them, change them or pretend to be you.

KEEP IT SECRET

- Do not tell your password to friends, even your best friend.
- The only people who may know it are the grown-ups who look after you and your computer, such as a parent or a teacher.
- Type it where nobody is watching your fingers.
- Do not write it on a paper stuck to the computer. If you must write it down, give the paper to the grown-up who looks after it.
- If you think someone else knows your password, tell a grown-up so it can be changed.

A GOOD PASSWORD

The United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA, "Use strong passwords") gives three rules: a password should be long, random and different for each account. It advises at least 16 characters, either a mix of letters, numbers and signs, or a passphrase of four to seven words that do not belong together.

- Long: four or more words that do not belong together, like "purple river spoon kite".
- Random: not your name, your birthday, your pet's name or "password". Those are the first things someone would try.
- Different: use a different password for each thing, so one found password does not open everything.

A grown-up can help you choose one and keep a copy safe.

STAYING SAFE WHEN YOU ARE ONLINE

When a computer is connected to the Internet, you can meet people you do not know. The United States Federal Trade Commission (FTC) gives this advice for children:

- Some people lie about who they really are, so you never really know who you are talking to.
- Keep private things private, such as your street address, your telephone number, and money or bank details.
- Ask before you share anything about someone else.
- Be careful if someone you met online wants to meet you in person, and tell a grown-up before you go anywhere.
- If someone or something online makes you feel uncomfortable, frightened or worried, stop and tell a grown-up you trust.

ON THIS COMPUTER

Even with no Internet, a password protects your own work from others who use the same computer. Log out when you finish, so the next person logs in with their own name.
