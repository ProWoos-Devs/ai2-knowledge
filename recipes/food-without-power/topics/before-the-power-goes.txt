Getting ready before the power goes

Search terms: prepare for power cut, before a blackout, storm coming, get ready, freezer full, ice packs, cool box, cooler, appliance thermometer, plan ahead.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Preparing the refrigerator and freezer before a power cut you expect, or for the next one

Not for: What to do once the power is off. See Keeping food cold during a power cut.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- A storm or a power cut is coming
- You want to be ready for the next one

WHAT TO DO

1. Keep an appliance thermometer in the refrigerator and one in the freezer. The refrigerator should be at 4 °C (40 °F) or below and the freezer at -18 °C (0 °F) or below. [USDA, CDC]

2. Keep the freezer full. Fill empty spaces with frozen bottles or jugs of water, bags of ice or gel packs. [USDA, CDC]

3. Group foods together in the refrigerator and in the freezer. They stay cold longer that way. [USDA]

4. Freeze refrigerator food you will not need soon, such as leftovers, milk and fresh meat and poultry. Frozen, it stays at a safe temperature longer. [USDA]

5. Have a large insulated cool box and frozen gel packs ready, in case food has to come out of the refrigerator. [USDA, CDC]

6. Find out where dry ice or block ice can be bought. [USDA]

DO NOT

- Do not leave empty space in the freezer. Fill it with frozen water, bags of ice or gel packs. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If a flood is expected, raise the refrigerator and freezer off the floor if you can, for example on cement blocks under the corners, and move canned goods and other food from basements and low cupboards to a higher place. [USDA]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Plastic jugs or bottles of water frozen in the freezer work as ice. [USDA, CDC]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Bags of ice or gel packs instead of frozen water. [USDA]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- USDA says to find out beforehand where dry ice and block ice can be bought. If none can be bought, the frozen water you made before the power went is the ice you have. [USDA]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A thermometer. Without one, use the closed-door times as a guide, and for thawing freezer food check for ice crystals. See Judging food with no thermometer. [CDC, USDA, Chart]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The sources give temperatures in Fahrenheit. The Celsius figures for the refrigerator come from the FoodSafety.gov chart; the freezer figure in Celsius is our own conversion.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Storing drinking water. See the Water and Sanitation Knowledge Pack.
