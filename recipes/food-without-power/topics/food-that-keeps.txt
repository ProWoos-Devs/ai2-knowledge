Food that keeps without a refrigerator

Search terms: what can I eat without a fridge, food that does not need refrigeration, long power cut, no electricity for a week, safe food without cold, bread, hard cheese, peanut butter, dried fruit, nuts, flour.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Planning meals when the refrigerator and freezer will stay off

Not for: Food that is already warm and may need throwing out. See Refrigerator food: keep or throw out.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The power will be off for a long time
- You want to know which foods are not at risk

WHAT TO DO

The sources list these foods as still safe after a power cut. None says how long they keep without a refrigerator, or in a hot place. Check each for mold and smell before every use. [USDA, Chart]

1. These foods do not need a refrigerator and can be used unless they turn moldy or smell unusual: whole fresh fruit and vegetables; unopened fruit and vegetable juices; dried fruit; bread, rolls, muffins and cakes without cream cheese frosting or cream filling; fruit pies; hard and processed cheeses; butter and margarine; fresh herbs and spices; flour; nuts. [USDA]

2. The chart also keeps peanut butter; jelly (fruit spread), relish, mustard, ketchup, olives and pickles; soy, Worcestershire and barbecue sauces; vinegar-based dressings; opened canned fruit; grated Parmesan in a can or jar; waffles, pancakes and bagels. [Chart]

3. High-acid foods such as mustard, ketchup, relishes, pickles, non-creamy dressings, jams and jellies are safe, but may spoil sooner. [USDA]

4. Check every can before opening it. The CDC's list, written for floods, says to throw out cans that are bulging, open or damaged, and any can that spurts liquid or foam when opened, or holds food that is discolored, moldy or smells bad. [CDC]

5. Some canned food must be kept cold. The chart throws out canned hams labeled "Keep Refrigerated" after 2 hours at 4 °C (40 °F) or above. [Chart]

6. Cooked food is not on these lists. The chart throws out leftover cooked meat, soups, stews, casseroles, cooked vegetables and cooked pasta, rice and potatoes after more than 2 hours at 4 °C (40 °F) or above. [Chart]

DO NOT

- Do not use any of these foods if they turn moldy or smell unusual. [USDA]

- Do not taste food to test it. [USDA, CDC]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- Once a can is opened, meat or fish in it becomes refrigerator food. The chart says to throw out opened canned meat and fish after 2 hours above 4 °C (40 °F). [Chart]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- Bread, dried fruit, nuts, hard cheese and whole fruit and vegetables need no refrigerator, but check them for mold and smell. [USDA]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Frozen bread, rolls, muffins, flour, nuts, waffles, pancakes and bagels can be used even after the freezer has warmed. [Chart]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- These are the foods to rely on once the refrigerator and freezer times have passed. [USDA, Chart]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- A can that is whole. Damaged cans are thrown out. [USDA, CDC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- USDA lists "dried fruits and coconut" as safe; the chart says throw out sliced or shredded coconut from the refrigerator.

- No source read says how long these foods last once the power is off for weeks. Check each for mold and smell before use.

- The sources do not list unopened shelf-stable cans as safe in so many words; they cover damaged cans, opened cans and cans labeled "Keep Refrigerated".

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Storing food for emergencies in advance, and drying or salting food. Not in the sources read.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
