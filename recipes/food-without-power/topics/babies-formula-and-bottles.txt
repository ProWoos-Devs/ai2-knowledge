Babies: formula, bottles and pacifiers

Search terms: baby formula after power cut, opened formula, infant milk, baby bottle, nipple, teat, pacifier, dummy, soother, flood, feeding a baby with no power.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Baby formula, bottles and pacifiers after a power cut, flood or fire

Not for: Breastfeeding, which none of the sources read covers.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The power was off and opened baby formula was in the refrigerator
- Floodwater reached formula, bottles or pacifiers

WHAT TO DO

1. Opened formula that was kept in the refrigerator: throw it out after 4 hours in a refrigerator with no power and no other cold source, or if you know it was at 4 °C (40 °F) or above for more than 2 hours. [Chart, CDC] The chart's row covers refrigerated formula only. It gives no rule for a bottle once a feed has started, or for an opened tin of dry powder.

2. Throw out formula in cardboard boxes that floodwater touched. [USDA, CDC]

3. Throw out baby bottle nipples (teats) and pacifiers (dummies, soothers) that floodwater may have touched. There is no way to clean them safely. [USDA, CDC]

4. Concentrated formula in an all-metal can that was washed, sanitized and dried after a flood must be mixed with clean drinking water. See Saving cans and pouches after a flood. [USDA]

5. Throw out any formula in a damaged can, or in a container with a screw cap, snap lid or other non-waterproof closure, that floodwater may have reached. [USDA, CDC]

DO NOT

- Do not taste formula to test it. [USDA, CDC]

- Do not mix formula with floodwater or with water you are not sure is safe. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- Throw out formula or baby food that was near a fire. See Food near a fire. [USDA]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- No source read covers making formula when there is no safe water. Use clean drinking water to mix concentrated formula. [USDA] The Water and Sanitation Knowledge Pack covers making water safe, and its topic What treatment cannot remove says which water no treatment makes safe.

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Undamaged, waterproof all-metal cans of formula can be washed, sanitized, dried and used as soon as possible. See Saving cans and pouches after a flood. [USDA] A tin with a plastic lid is not all-metal; that note is ours.

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- When in doubt, throw it out. It applies to a baby's food as to everyone's. [USDA, CDC]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Clean drinking water for mixing formula. [USDA]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- No source read says how long unopened formula keeps without power, or how to clean bottles. They are not covered here.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Keeping babies warm. See the Staying Warm Safely Knowledge Pack.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
