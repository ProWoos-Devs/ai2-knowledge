Keeping food cold during a power cut

Search terms: how long does food last in the fridge without power, how long will the freezer stay cold, 4 hours, 48 hours, keep the door shut, dry ice, block ice, snow, make ice outside, blackout.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: The hours while the power is off

Not for: Deciding what to throw out afterwards. See Refrigerator food: keep or throw out, and Freezer food: refreeze or throw out.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The power is off and food is still in the refrigerator or freezer

WHAT TO DO

1. Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed. Open them as little as possible. [USDA, CDC, Chart]

2. With the doors closed, the refrigerator keeps food safe for up to 4 hours. [USDA, CDC]

3. A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours. A half-full one holds it for about 24 hours. [USDA, CDC]

4. If the power will be off for a long time, get dry ice or block ice. USDA says 50 pounds (about 23 kg) of dry ice should hold an 18 cubic foot (about 510 liter) freezer for 2 days. [USDA]

5. In a freezer, food at the front, in the door or in small thin packages thaws faster than large thick items or food at the back or bottom. Check those first. [USDA]

6. After 4 hours without power, move perishable refrigerator food to a cool box with ice. See Moving food to a cool box. [CDC]

DO NOT

- Do not touch dry ice with bare hands, and do not let it touch food. [USDA]

- In a snowstorm, do not put perishable food outside in the snow. Outside temperatures change, and animals and dirt can reach it. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the power comes back, check the thermometers before deciding about any food. See Refrigerator food: keep or throw out. [CDC]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- When it is freezing outside, make ice instead of putting food out. Fill buckets, empty milk containers or cans with water and leave them outside to freeze. Use that ice in the refrigerator, freezer or cool box. [USDA]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Block ice or bags of ice instead of dry ice. [USDA, CDC]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Once the refrigerator's 4 hours have passed with no ice, throw out what the chart says to throw out. For the freezer, the 24 and 48 hours are how long it holds its temperature; after that, judge each food by ice crystals or temperature. See Freezer food: refreeze or throw out, and Food that keeps without a refrigerator. [USDA, CDC, Chart]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Closed doors. Every source tells you to keep them closed. [USDA, CDC, Chart]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The amount of dry ice is given only by USDA, in pounds and cubic feet. The metric figures are our own conversion.

- No source read says how long a refrigerator stays safe with ice inside it. Check with a thermometer if you have one.

- No source read gives a way to keep food cold without ice, dry ice or freezing weather.

- The sources give no dry ice precautions beyond not touching it and not letting it touch food.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Generators. See the Staying Warm Safely Knowledge Pack for where a generator may be run.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
