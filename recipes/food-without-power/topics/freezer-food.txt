Freezer food: refreeze or throw out

Search terms: freezer thawed, can I refreeze, food defrosted, ice crystals, freezer was off, frozen meat thawed, ice cream melted, refreeze after power cut.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Deciding about each food in the freezer after the power was off

Not for: Refrigerator food. See Refrigerator food: keep or throw out.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The freezer has been without power and some food may have thawed

WHAT TO DO

1. Judge each item that has started to thaw. Food that still has ice crystals, or is at 4 °C (40 °F) or below, can be refrozen or cooked. Its quality may suffer. [CDC, USDA, Chart] The chart throws out ice cream and frozen yogurt that has started to thaw, even if it still has ice crystals. [Chart]

2. If you know a food has been at 4 °C (40 °F) or above for more than 2 hours: throw out meat, poultry and seafood; stews and soups; milk; eggs out of the shell and egg products; soft, semi-soft and shredded cheeses; cheesecake; cakes, pies and pastries with custard or cheese filling; pasta and rice casseroles; frozen meals and pizza. [Chart]

3. These can be refrozen even after 2 hours above 4 °C (40 °F): hard cheeses; bread, rolls, muffins and cakes without custard filling; pie crusts and bread dough, with a big loss of quality; flour, cornmeal and nuts; waffles, pancakes and bagels. [Chart]

4. Fruit and fruit juice can be refrozen even after 2 hours above 4 °C (40 °F), unless mold, a yeasty smell or sliminess appears. [Chart]

5. Vegetables and vegetable juice: throw out once they have been above 4 °C (40 °F) for 6 hours. [Chart]

6. Throw out ice cream and frozen yogurt that has thawed, even if it still has ice crystals. [Chart]

DO NOT

- Do not taste thawed food to decide. [USDA, Chart]

- Do not refreeze ice cream or frozen yogurt that has started to thaw. [Chart]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the power comes back while food still has ice crystals, refreeze it, except ice cream and frozen yogurt. [Chart]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- With no thermometer, the chart's test is food that "contains ice crystals and feels cold as if refrigerated". [Chart]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Thawed food that still has ice crystals can be cooked instead of refrozen. [CDC] This does not cover ice cream and frozen yogurt that has started to thaw, which the chart throws out. [Chart]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- A full, closed freezer holds about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24. [USDA, CDC] Small thin packages and food in the door or at the front thaw sooner, so judge each item by ice crystals or temperature, not by the clock. [USDA, Chart]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- For perishable frozen food, ice crystals or a thermometer reading of 4 °C (40 °F) or below. [CDC, Chart] The foods in steps 3 and 4 are the chart's exceptions.

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The chart's refreezing test (ice crystals, or 4 °C (40 °F) or below) and its throw-out column (4 °C (40 °F) or above for more than 2 hours) both include 4 °C. Our reading is that the throw-out column applies when you know how long the food was warm, and the refreezing test applies to what you find now. If you cannot tell that a perishable food is safe, throw it out. [Chart, USDA]

- The USDA page lists foods that do not need freezing (dried fruit, baked goods without cream filling, hard cheeses, butter, fruit juices, nuts) as usable unless moldy or smelling unusual. The chart agrees for those it lists.

- Once thawed food is cooked, it is cooked food with no refrigerator. The chart throws out leftover cooked meat, poultry and fish, soups, stews, casseroles, cooked vegetables, and cooked pasta, rice and potatoes after more than 2 hours at 4 °C (40 °F) or above. [Chart]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Freezing food in winter outdoors. USDA advises against putting food in the snow. See Keeping food cold during a power cut.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
