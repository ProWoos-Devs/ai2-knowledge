Start here: food when the power is off

Search terms: power cut, power outage, blackout, electricity off, fridge stopped, freezer stopped, is my food still safe, where do I start, no power for days, food safety.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Households keeping food safe when the refrigerator and freezer have no power

Not for: Shops, restaurants or food banks. The sources are written for homes.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The power has gone off, or is about to
- You do not know which topic to read first

WHAT TO DO

1. Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed. With the doors closed, food stays safe for up to 4 hours in a refrigerator, about 48 hours in a full freezer and about 24 hours in a half-full one. [CDC, USDA] See Keeping food cold during a power cut.

2. Note the time the power went off. The 4 hours, 24 hours and 48 hours are counted from then. [CDC]

3. If the power is still off after 4 hours, move perishable refrigerator food into a cool box with ice, if you have them. See Moving food to a cool box. [CDC]

4. When you must decide about a food, see Refrigerator food: keep or throw out, and Freezer food: refreeze or throw out. [Chart]

5. Never taste food to find out if it is safe. When in doubt, throw it out. [USDA, CDC]

DO NOT

- Do not taste food to test it. [USDA, CDC]

- Do not put food outside in the snow to keep it cold. See Keeping food cold during a power cut. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If floodwater reached the food, see Food touched by floodwater. [USDA, CDC]

- If there was a fire nearby, see Food near a fire. [USDA]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- With no thermometer, see Judging food with no thermometer.

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Many foods do not need a refrigerator at all. See Food that keeps without a refrigerator. [USDA, Chart]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- If the power stays off for days and there is no ice, the refrigerator times above have passed. Rely on the food that keeps without a refrigerator, and throw out what the charts say to throw out. [USDA, Chart]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Cold. No source read gives a way to make perishable food safe once it has been warm for too long. Cooking it does not appear in any of them as a fix.

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- The USDA page (2013) and the FoodSafety.gov chart (reviewed 2024) differ on a few foods. These topics follow the newer chart and note each difference.

- No source read covers cooking with no power. For stoves, grills and carbon monoxide, see the Staying Warm Safely Knowledge Pack.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Drinking water. See the Water and Sanitation Knowledge Pack.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
