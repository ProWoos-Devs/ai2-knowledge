Refrigerator food: keep or throw out

Search terms: what to throw out after power cut, fridge was off, is the milk ok, are eggs safe, cheese, meat, leftovers, mayonnaise, butter, which food to keep, power came back, fridge warm.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Deciding about each food in the refrigerator after the power was off

Not for: Frozen food. See Freezer food: refreeze or throw out.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The refrigerator has been without power for more than 4 hours, or food in it has been at 4 °C (40 °F) or above for more than 2 hours [USDA, CDC, Chart]

WHAT TO DO

1. Decide food by food. Never taste anything to test it. [Chart, USDA]

2. Throw out: raw or cooked meat, poultry, fish and seafood, and soy meat substitutes; thawing meat; lunch meats (sliced cooked meats), hot dogs, bacon, sausage and dried beef; canned hams labeled "Keep Refrigerated"; pizza; casseroles, soups, stews, gravy, stuffing and broth; meat, fish, chicken or egg salads; opened canned meat or fish. [Chart]

3. Throw out: milk, cream, sour cream, buttermilk, evaporated milk, yogurt and soy milk; soft cheeses, shredded cheeses and low-fat cheeses (the chart counts as soft: blue, Roquefort, Brie, Camembert, cottage, cream cheese, Edam, Monterey Jack, ricotta, mozzarella, Muenster, Neufchatel, queso blanco and queso fresco); opened baby formula; eggs, egg dishes, custards, puddings and quiche. [Chart]

4. Throw out: cut fresh fruit and vegetables; cooked vegetables; cooked pasta, rice and potatoes; pasta salads and potato salad; batters and doughs; sliced or shredded coconut; fresh pasta; packaged pre-cut greens; cooked tofu; opened vegetable juice; garlic in oil; opened spaghetti sauce; opened creamy dressings; fish sauce and oyster sauce; refrigerator dough; cheesecake; cream-filled pastries; pies with egg or milk filling. [Chart]

5. Throw out opened mayonnaise, tartar sauce and horseradish if they were above 10 °C (50 °F) for more than 8 hours. [Chart, USDA]

6. Keep: hard cheeses (the chart lists cheddar, Colby, Swiss, Parmesan, provolone and Romano); processed cheeses; grated Parmesan in a can or jar; butter and margarine; uncut fresh fruit and vegetables; opened fruit juice; opened canned fruit; dried fruit; peanut butter; jelly (fruit spread), relish, mustard, ketchup, olives and pickles; soy, Worcestershire and barbecue sauces; opened vinegar-based dressings; bread, rolls, cakes and muffins; waffles, pancakes and bagels; fruit pies; fresh mushrooms, herbs and spices. [Chart]

7. Throw out anything with an unusual smell, color or texture, whatever the list says. [CDC, USDA]

8. Throw out food that raw meat, poultry or fish juices may have dripped on, such as bread or salad greens. [USDA]

DO NOT

- Do not taste food to decide. [USDA, CDC]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If food went into a cool box with ice, check its temperature. CDC says to throw out food that has thawed or is above 4 °C (40 °F); the chart's lists are for more than 2 hours at that temperature. [CDC, Chart]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- With no thermometer, see Judging food with no thermometer.

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- See Food that keeps without a refrigerator for what to eat once the perishable food is gone.

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- The list does not change when help is far away. Food that the chart says to throw out can make people sick even if it looks and smells normal. [CDC]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Throwing out the unsafe food. No source read gives a way to make it safe again.

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Opened fruit juice: the USDA page says throw it out, the newer FoodSafety.gov chart says keep it. This topic follows the chart.

- Coconut: USDA lists "dried fruits and coconut" as safe, the chart says throw out sliced or shredded coconut. This topic follows the chart.

- The USDA page, the older source, says high-acid foods such as mustard, ketchup, pickles, non-creamy dressings and jams are safe but "may spoil sooner". [USDA]

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Food in a refrigerator that floodwater reached. See Food touched by floodwater.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
