Food near a fire

Search terms: fire in the house, smoke, kitchen fire, food after fire, fumes, fire extinguisher chemicals, canned food after fire, fridge after a fire, smoke damage food.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Food and cookware after a fire in or near the home

Not for: Heating and fire safety. See the Staying Warm Safely Knowledge Pack.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- There has been a fire in or near the place food is kept

WHAT TO DO

1. Throw out all food that has been near a fire. Heat, smoke fumes and fire-fighting chemicals can damage it, and the fumes and chemicals cannot be washed off. [USDA]

2. That includes food kept outside the refrigerator, such as bread, fruit and vegetables. [USDA]

3. It includes raw food and food in packaging that lets air through, such as cardboard or plastic wrap. [USDA]

4. It includes all food in cans, bottles and jars. They may look fine, but the heat can damage the containers and start spoilage. [USDA]

5. It includes food in the refrigerator and freezer. Their seals are not airtight and fumes can get in. [USDA]

6. Wash cookware that fire-fighting chemicals reached in soap and hot water, then soak it for 15 minutes in 1 tablespoon (about 15 mL) of unscented liquid chlorine bleach per US gallon (about 3.8 liters) of drinking water. [USDA]

DO NOT

- Do not keep canned or bottled food because it looks undamaged. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If floodwater from fire fighting also reached the kitchen, see Food touched by floodwater as well. [USDA, CDC]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- No source read gives a way to clean cookware after a fire without bleach.

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- None in the sources. The food is thrown out. [USDA]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- USDA gives no exception for when other food is hard to get: food that was near the fire is thrown out. [USDA]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Throwing the food out. The chemicals cannot be washed off. [USDA]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- Only USDA covers fires. It does not say how near "near a fire" is. The metric figures for the bleach solution are our own conversion.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Smoke inhalation and burns. See the First Aid Knowledge Pack.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
