Moving food to a cool box

Search terms: cooler, cool box, ice chest, after 4 hours, put food on ice, gel packs, keep food cold without fridge, esky, transfer food.

Written from USDA, FoodSafety.gov and CDC sources. Not reviewed by a food safety specialist.
Last reviewed: 2026-11-27

For: Perishable refrigerator food when the power has been off for 4 hours

Not for: Frozen food in a closed freezer, which lasts longer. See Keeping food cold during a power cut.

USE THIS TOPIC WHEN

- The power has been off for 4 hours, and you have a cool box and ice or frozen gel packs

WHAT TO DO

1. Move perishable refrigerator food into the cool box: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, cut fruit and vegetables, and leftovers. [CDC, USDA]

2. Fill it with ice or frozen gel packs. Use enough ice to keep the food at 4 °C (40 °F) or below. [USDA, CDC]

3. Add more ice as it melts. [USDA]

4. If you have a thermometer, check the temperature of the food in the cool box. Throw out food that has thawed or is above 4 °C (40 °F). [CDC]

DO NOT

- Do not keep perishable food in a refrigerator that has had no power and no ice for more than 4 hours. [CDC, USDA]

- Do not let dry ice touch food or bare skin. [USDA]

IF THINGS CHANGE

- If the ice runs out and the food warms above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours, see Refrigerator food: keep or throw out. [Chart]

WITH NO SUPPLIES

- In freezing weather, water left outside in buckets, milk containers or cans makes ice for the cool box. [USDA]

ALTERNATIVES USING WHAT IS AVAILABLE

- Frozen bottles or jugs of water from the freezer work as ice. [USDA, CDC]

- Ice can also go into the refrigerator itself instead of a cool box. [CDC]

IF HELP IS DELAYED OR CANNOT BE REACHED

- Without new ice, a cool box only gives extra time. Once the food has been above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours, throw out what the chart says to throw out, and rely on food that keeps without a refrigerator. [Chart, USDA]

WHAT HAS NO EFFECTIVE SUBSTITUTE

- Enough ice. Food in a cool box is only safe while it stays at 4 °C (40 °F) or below. [USDA, CDC]

WHERE SOURCES DIFFER OR ARE SILENT

- No source read says how much ice a cool box needs or how long it lasts.

- CDC says to throw out cool-box food found above 4 °C (40 °F). The chart's lists are for food at 4 °C (40 °F) or above for more than 2 hours. Both rules are given as each source states them.

LEFT OFF THIS TOPIC

- Carrying food in a car or keeping it on a journey. Not in the sources read.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies, last updated 2013 (USDA); FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During Power Outage charts, reviewed 2024 (Chart); CDC, Keep Food Safe After a Disaster or Emergency, 2025 (CDC).
