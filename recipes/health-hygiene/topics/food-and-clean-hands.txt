Keeping food safe to eat

Search terms: food safety, food poisoning, clean hands, raw food, cooked food, leftovers, no refrigerator, food left out, clean plate, wash vegetables, raw dough.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

FOOD CAN CARRY GERMS

Food poisoning can happen when food carries harmful germs. Riskier foods include raw or undercooked meat, poultry, seafood, eggs and sprouts; unwashed produce; cut melon; and raw dough made with uncooked flour. They also include unpasteurized milk, juice, cider and soft cheese. Unpasteurized means not given the heat treatment that kills harmful germs; milk straight from an animal is unpasteurized. [FOOD, Overview and Riskier foods; MILK, Overview; web.]

START WITH CLEAN HANDS

Wash your hands with soap and water before eating and before preparing food. Scrub for at least 20 seconds, rinse and dry. Wash again after handling raw meat, seafood, eggs or flour. Use clean utensils and work surfaces. Rinse fresh fruits and vegetables with safe running water. Water that may be unsafe should not be used to wash or prepare food. [FOOD, Wash your hands and surfaces often and Reminder; HAND, How it works; WATER, Overview; web.]

KEEP RAW FOOD SEPARATE

Juice from raw meat can carry germs onto food that will not be cooked. Keep raw meat, poultry and seafood away from ready-to-eat food. Use a separate board or plate for raw animal foods and for food such as bread or vegetables that will be eaten without cooking. A plate that just held raw chicken is not a clean plate for a cooked meal. Adults can keep raw food in closed containers so juices do not leak. [FOOD, Separate foods to avoid cross-contamination; web.]

COOKING NEEDS THE RIGHT CHECK

For adults cooking food: use a food thermometer to check the temperature inside. The rounded Celsius equivalents of CDC's temperatures are:

- All poultry, including ground chicken or turkey: about 74 °C.
- Leftovers and casseroles: about 74 °C.
- Ground beef or pork: about 71 °C.
- Whole cuts of beef, veal, lamb or pork: about 63 °C, then rest for 3 minutes before cutting or eating.
- Fish with fins: about 63 °C, or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork. Opaque means you cannot see through it. [FOOD, Safe internal temperatures for different foods; web.]

Color and texture cannot show that meat or poultry is safely cooked. There is no check without a thermometer given here for these foods. Do not taste raw dough or batter containing uncooked flour or eggs. [FOOD, Cook to the right temperature and Riskier foods; web.]

LEFTOVERS AND NO REFRIGERATOR

Perishable foods, such as meat, milk, cut fruit and cooked leftovers, can become unsafe without suitable cooling. Refrigerate them within 2 hours, or within 1 hour when the surrounding temperature is above about 32 °C. Keep the refrigerator at about 4 °C or colder. [FOOD, Refrigerate food promptly; web.]

Without refrigeration, do not eat or keep perishable food that has been left out for more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour in heat above about 32 °C. A cover does not keep food cold. This Knowledge Pack does not explain keeping cooked leftovers overnight without cooling. [FOOD, Refrigerate food promptly; web.]
