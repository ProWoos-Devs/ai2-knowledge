Keeping food safe to eat

Search terms: food safety, food poisoning, clean hands, raw food, cooked food, leftovers, no refrigerator, food left out, clean plate, wash vegetables, raw dough.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

FOOD CAN CARRY GERMS

Food poisoning can happen when food carries harmful germs. Raw meat, poultry, seafood and eggs can carry germs. So can unwashed produce and raw dough made with uncooked flour. Cooking and clean handling matter; food does not become safe just because it looks ready to eat. This topic explains everyday habits, not a complete cooking or food-preservation method. [FOOD, Overview, Riskier foods and Cook to the right temperature; web.]

START WITH CLEAN HANDS

Wash your hands with soap and water before eating and before preparing food. Scrub for at least 20 seconds, rinse and dry. Wash again after handling raw meat, seafood, eggs or flour. Use clean utensils and work surfaces. Rinse fresh fruits and vegetables with safe running water. Water that may be unsafe should not be used to wash or prepare food. [FOOD, Wash your hands and surfaces often and Reminder; HAND, How it works; WATER, Overview; web.]

KEEP RAW FOOD SEPARATE

Juice from raw meat can carry germs onto food that will not be cooked. Keep raw meat, poultry and seafood away from ready-to-eat food. Use a separate board or plate for raw animal foods and for food such as bread or vegetables that will be eaten without cooking. A plate that just held raw chicken is not a clean plate for a cooked meal. Adults can keep raw food in closed containers so juices do not leak. [FOOD, Separate foods to avoid cross-contamination; web.]

COOKING NEEDS THE RIGHT CHECK

An adult preparing food needs to cook it thoroughly. Meat or poultry being brown on the outside does not show that the inside reached a safe temperature. A food thermometer checks that; this topic does not replace the appropriate temperature instructions for each food. Do not taste raw dough or batter containing uncooked flour or eggs. [FOOD, Riskier foods and Cook to the right temperature; web.]

LEFTOVERS AND NO REFRIGERATOR

Perishable foods are foods that can become unsafe without suitable temperature control, such as meat, milk, cut fruit and cooked leftovers. They need prompt refrigeration: within 2 hours, or within 1 hour when the surrounding temperature is above about 32 °C. The refrigerator should be about 4 °C or colder. These are limits for ordinary storage, not promises that an unknown food is safe until a timer rings. [FOOD, Refrigerate food promptly; web.]

Without a refrigerator or another way to keep food at a verified safe temperature, a covered bowl in a room does not provide the cooling the guidance requires. Ask an adult about food that has been left out. This source gives no general method for keeping cooked leftovers safely overnight without cooling. Covering food helps with handling; it does not replace temperature control. [FOOD, Refrigerate food promptly and Cook to the right temperature; web.]
