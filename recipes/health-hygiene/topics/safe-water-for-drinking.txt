Safe water for drinking

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

LOOKING CLEAR IS NOT A SAFETY TEST

After a flood, broken supply or other emergency, tap water may be unavailable or unsafe. Water can need treatment even when it looks clear. A child cannot establish that water is safe just by looking at it. Safe water matters for drinking, preparing food, washing dishes and brushing teeth. The same stored water that is safe to drink can be used for these tasks. [WATER, Overview and Boil; STORE, Bottled water; web.]

WHERE DRINKING WATER COMES FROM

During an emergency, an unopened bottle of commercially bottled water is a useful safe source if it is available. Other water may need correct treatment before use. Ask the adult responsible for the supply which water is ready to drink. This topic explains protecting drinking water; it does not provide children with boiling instructions, treatment doses or a way to check an unknown treatment. [STORE, Bottled water; WATER, Overview; web.]

KEEP SAFE WATER CLEAN

Keep drinking water in a clean container with a tight cover. A narrow opening makes it possible to pour water without putting a hand inside. When taking water out, do not touch the water or the inside of the container with your hands. If a scoop is used, it must be clean each time. Previously safe water can be contaminated during handling. [STORE, Choosing a container and Using the water; web.]

A container that once held bleach, pesticides or another poisonous chemical is not suitable for drinking water, even if it now looks empty. For adults choosing storage containers, use a container made for food or water, with a secure lid and durable material. Keep stored drinking water away from toxic substances and direct sunlight. Label it so the household knows it is drinking water. [STORE, Choosing a container, Don't use unsafe containers and Storing the water; web.]

WHAT BOILING CANNOT FIX

Boiling or disinfecting cannot make water containing fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive material safe to drink. Such water needs a different safe source; extra boiling is not the answer. Do not use water from a house-heating radiator or heating-system boiler for drinking or cooking. It may contain substances that make people ill. [WATER, Overview and Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it; web.]

WHEN THERE IS NO SAFE SUPPLY

A parent or teacher may need to find another source or use a complete, appropriate water-treatment method. This topic does not make an unknown supply safe, and it offers no improvised chemical dose. If safe water, treatment equipment and help are all unavailable, that is a real gap: the sources used here do not establish a universal safe alternative for every possible kind of contaminated water. The storage habits above still help protect any safe water the household obtains. [WATER, Overview and Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it; STORE, Using the water; web.]
