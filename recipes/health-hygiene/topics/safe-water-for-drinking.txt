Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

LOOKING CLEAR IS NOT A SAFETY TEST

After a flood, broken supply or other emergency, tap water may be unavailable or unsafe. Water can need treatment even when it looks clear. A child cannot establish that water is safe just by looking at it. Safe water matters for drinking, preparing food, washing dishes and brushing teeth. The same stored water that is safe to drink can be used for these tasks. [WATER, Overview and Boil; STORE, Bottled water; web.]

WHERE DRINKING WATER COMES FROM

During an emergency, an unopened bottle of commercially bottled water is a useful safe source if it is available. Other water may need treatment before use. Ask the adult responsible for the supply which water is ready to drink. [STORE, Bottled water; WATER, Overview; web.]

BOILING: FOR PARENTS AND TEACHERS

For an adult preparing drinking water: heat the water until it is bubbling hard, with large bubbles breaking all over the surface without stopping. This is called a rolling boil. The first small bubbles rising from the bottom are not enough. Once the water is bubbling hard, take it off the heat. It does not need to keep boiling, and this also works at high altitude. Let it cool on its own, then store it in a clean container with a tight cover. [BOIL p. 1, Introduction and Conclusions; YELLOW, Heat; WATER, Steps for boiling water; web.]

If water is cloudy, first pass it through a clean cloth, or let it settle and pour off the clear water; then boil it. Boiling cannot make water containing fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive material safe: use a different safe source. [WATER, Steps for boiling water and Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it; web.]

KEEP SAFE WATER CLEAN

Keep drinking water in a clean container with a tight cover. A narrow opening makes it possible to pour water without putting a hand inside. When taking water out, do not touch the water or the inside of the container with your hands. If a scoop is used, it must be clean each time. Previously safe water can be contaminated during handling. [STORE, Choosing a container and Using the water; web.]

A container that once held bleach, pesticides or another poisonous chemical is not suitable for drinking water, even if it now looks empty. Adults should choose a durable container made for food or water, with a secure lid. Keep stored drinking water away from toxic substances and direct sunlight. Label it as drinking water. [STORE, Choosing a container, Don't use unsafe containers and Storing the water; web.]

WHEN THERE IS NO SAFE SUPPLY

If there is no safe supply and no way to treat water, this Knowledge Pack has no safe replacement to offer. It gives no chemical treatment doses. Boiling kills germs; it does not remove every kind of pollution. [WATER, Overview and Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it; web.]
