Staying home and returning to group learning

Search terms: stay home, sick child, school attendance, return to school, return after illness, fever, vomiting, diarrhea, rash, draining sores, group learning, parents, teachers, twenty-four hours.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

FOR PARENTS AND TEACHERS

These are CDC's general school guidelines for deciding when a child should stay away from group learning and when to return. Staying home is a separate question from needing urgent medical help. The topic “When you feel ill” gives the urgent signs; this topic is about attendance, not emergency assessment. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick and Return to School; FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; web.]

WHEN A CHILD SHOULD STAY HOME

Keep a child home from group learning with fever, including fever with a new rash; vomiting more than twice in the previous 24 hours; diarrhea causing accidents, containing blood or causing more than 2 extra bowel movements above the child's usual number in 24 hours; draining skin sores that cannot be covered; or breathing-related symptoms that worsen or fail to improve, unless another cause such as allergies explains them. A child also needs to stay home if unable to take part comfortably or needing more care than staff can provide. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; web.]

WHEN A CHILD CAN RETURN

Returning depends on the illness and recovery. The child should be well enough to take part, and their care needs should be manageable by the staff. CDC's general school guidance requires at least 24 hours without fever and without fever-reducing medicine. Fever with a new rash needs medical assessment, and the fever must have resolved before returning. Bloody diarrhea also needs medical assessment before returning. [SICK, Return to School; web.]

After vomiting, return when it has stopped overnight and the child can keep down food or liquids in the morning. Diarrhea must have improved; CDC's criteria are no more accidents or no more than 2 extra bowel movements above the child's usual daily number. Breathing-related symptoms should be improving overall for at least 24 hours. Uncovered sores must be crusting and under medical treatment. [SICK, Return to School; web.]

Meeting a school rule does not mean every illness is cured. If a required medical assessment or treatment is unavailable, this Knowledge Pack cannot say that returning is safe. Different illnesses can need different return decisions. [SICK, Return to School; web.]

REDUCE SPREAD WHILE RECOVERING

Wash hands after the toilet and after coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose. Cover coughs with a tissue or elbow. [HAND, Key times to wash hands; COUGH, Steps you can take; web.]
