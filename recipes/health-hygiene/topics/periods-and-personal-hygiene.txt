Periods and personal hygiene

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

A NORMAL BODY PROCESS

A period, also called menstruation, is a normal body process. Blood and tissue from the lining of the uterus leave the body through the vagina. The uterus is an organ inside the lower body; the vagina is the passage the blood comes through. The outside genital area is called the vulva. Knowing these words helps you understand which part needs washing. A period does not mean that a person is dirty. [PERIOD, Overview and Other hygiene practices; web.]

PRODUCTS THAT ABSORB OR COLLECT BLOOD

Menstrual products include sanitary pads, period underwear, tampons and menstrual cups. They work in different ways and have different cleaning or changing instructions. This topic concentrates on pads and basic external hygiene; it does not teach inserting products. A helping adult can explain the available product and its directions. If using tampons, change them every 4 to 8 hours and never leave one in for more than 8 hours. Other products have different instructions; do not use a tampon time limit for a pad or cup. [PERIOD, Using menstrual products; web.]

Use the lowest tampon absorbency needed, meaning the least blood-holding capacity that meets your needs. Tampons are linked to a rare, serious illness called toxic shock syndrome. If sudden fever, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness, fainting or a sunburn-like rash occurs during or soon after your period, stop using tampons, tell a helping adult and seek medical help immediately. [TAMPON, Can tampons cause toxic shock syndrome (TSS)? and Tampon Safety Tips, tips 5 and 7; web.]

Wash your hands before and after changing a menstrual product. Change a sanitary pad every few hours, even when bleeding is light, and more often when the flow is heavy. Wearing one pad or pair of period underwear too long can cause skin irritation or infection. Wrap used disposable pads in paper or another wrapping material and put them in the trash. Do not flush menstrual products down the toilet. [PERIOD, Using menstrual products and Other hygiene practices; web.]

WASH THE OUTSIDE

Wash the vulva and bottom daily. Use only water to rinse the vulva. The vagina cleans itself inside: do not wash inside it or put cleaning chemicals there. When wiping after the toilet, move from front toward back. Choose unscented products because perfume can irritate the skin. Light, breathable clothing, such as cotton underwear, can help avoid trapping moisture. [PERIOD, Other hygiene practices; web.]

NOTICE CHANGES AND ASK FOR HELP

You can mark period dates on an ordinary calendar; an app or Internet connection is not needed. Tell a helping adult about extreme or unusual pain, a change in vaginal odor, or a period that is much heavier or longer than usual. These changes need medical assessment. This Knowledge Pack gives no pain-medicine doses. [PERIOD, Track and monitor your period and When to seek care; web.]

WHEN PRODUCTS, WATER OR DISPOSAL ARE MISSING

Reusable products have different washing instructions from disposable pads. This Knowledge Pack does not explain making or cleaning reusable cloth pads, washing with no water or disposing of pads without a waste system. A missing supply is a real problem, not a child's fault. [PERIOD, Using menstrual products; web.]
