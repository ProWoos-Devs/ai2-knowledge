Eating a variety of foods

Search terms: food, healthy eating, nutrition, nutrients, growing, energy, beans, vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein, food allergy, healthy snacks, not enough food.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

FOOD HELPS YOU GROW AND LEARN

Food supplies energy and nutrients, the useful substances a body needs to grow and work. Eating a range of foods helps children grow, learn and build strong bones and muscles. No single food does every job. Variety means choosing different kinds over time, rather than expecting every meal to contain every possible food. A healthy habit is something you practice regularly, not a test you pass by eating one special meal. [EAT, opening paragraphs and Healthy Eating; web.]

DIFFERENT FOODS CAN BELONG TO THE SAME GROUP

Vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein foods and suitable dairy foods or fortified alternatives can contribute different nutrients. Protein foods include beans, peas, lentils, eggs, fish, meat, nuts and soy foods. Beans can contribute to a meal even when meat is unavailable. Whole grains include foods made using the whole grain rather than only part of it. A food being expensive or advertised does not make it essential. [EAT, What should my child eat and drink? and Serve nutrient-rich foods and beverages; web.]

For example, a meal can include a grain food, beans and vegetables. It is an example, not a complete diet for every child. Available foods, allergies and what a child can tolerate matter. A parent or teacher can help choose among the foods the household actually has. The guidance encourages offering varied nutritious foods without making children feel guilty about a food choice. [EAT, Talk about being healthy, What should my child eat and drink? and Serve nutrient-rich foods and beverages; web.]

DRINKS AND SNACKS

Choose safe drinking water more often than sugary drinks. Fruit provides more fiber than fruit juice; fiber is material in plant foods that the body does not fully digest. Snacks can help supply nutrients as well as meals. Examples include fruit, vegetables and whole-grain bread. Choose foods you can safely eat: a food allergy is a reason an otherwise ordinary food may not suit you. [EAT, Think about the drink, Offer healthy snacks and What should my child eat and drink?; web.]

Frequent sugary snacks and drinks also give some mouth bacteria repeated chances to make tooth-damaging acid. Limit sweets and sugary drinks, rather than replacing ordinary meals with them. Food advice should not turn into teasing someone about their body or making them feel bad about limited food choices. [DECAY, Keep an eye on how often your child eats, as well as what she eats; EAT, Talk about being healthy and How can I help my child with his or her weight?; web.]

WHEN CHOICES ARE LIMITED

The source describes ways of improving choices among available foods. It does not supply a universal diet for a household with too little food, an allergy treatment, or a reason for a growing child to follow a weight-loss diet. This topic gives no calorie target or substitute recipe for missing nutrients. Parents and teachers should focus on nourishment and the child's ability to eat, not a number on a scale. [EAT, Healthy Eating and How can I help my child with his or her weight?; web.]
