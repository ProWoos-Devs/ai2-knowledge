Medicines and household cleaners

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

MEDICINE IS NOT A SWEET

Some medicines and vitamins look like candy, and some supplements are made as gummies. Their appearance does not make them safe to eat like sweets. Do not choose a medicine or dose without instructions for you. Usually a caregiver gives your medicine. If your health professional and caregiver have agreed that you can use a particular prescribed medicine yourself and taught you how, follow that personal plan. This Knowledge Pack does not replace it. An adult should explain what a medicine is for without calling it candy to persuade a child to take it. [ASTHMA pp. 13–14; MEDS, Keep young children safe; web.]

The rule includes vitamins and other supplements, not just prescription pills. You cannot reliably identify a medicine from its color or shape. If a child might have taken a medicine, vitamin or supplement that was not meant for them, tell an adult and seek help immediately, even if you are not completely sure. [MEDS, Keep young children safe and Medicine storage safety tips; web.]

ADULT STORAGE RESPONSIBILITIES

Adults should keep medicines and supplements out of young children's sight and reach. Put them away after each use, including between doses when a child is ill. Close a locking cap fully. Visitors' bags and coats can contain medicine too, so those also need safe storage. Keep other medicines away from young children while making prescribed rescue medicine available as the child's own care plan requires. This is a task for adults, not a reason for children to climb up and rearrange bottles. [ASTHMA pp. 13–14; MEDS, Medicine storage safety tips; web.]

CLEANING AND DISINFECTING ARE DIFFERENT

Cleaning uses soap or detergent, water and rubbing to remove dirt and germs. Disinfectants are products that kill germs left on surfaces. In most ordinary situations, cleaning with soap and water removes most germs; every surface does not automatically need bleach. A disinfectant for a table is not the same product as a hand cleaner. Use products only for their labeled purpose. [BLEACH, Overview and Definitions; HANDFAQ, Do wipes remove germs?; web.]

Never mix bleach or another disinfectant with other cleaners or disinfectants. The mixture can release dangerous fumes into the air. More products together do not mean better cleaning. An adult using a product must follow its directions and any instructions for letting fresh air in and using protective equipment. This Knowledge Pack gives no chemical-mixing recipes. [BLEACH, Important and Before using bleach; web.]

NOT FOR SKIN OR DRINKING

Do not use surface-disinfecting wipes to clean your skin; they can irritate it. Drinking-water containers must not be old containers that held bleach, pesticides or other toxic chemicals. A chemical bottle being empty does not make it a drinking bottle. [HANDFAQ, Do wipes remove germs?; STORE, Don't use unsafe containers; web.]

This Knowledge Pack gives no medicine doses or first aid for chemical exposure. A possible medicine poisoning needs help immediately, even if help is difficult to reach. [MEDS, Medicine storage safety tips; web.]
