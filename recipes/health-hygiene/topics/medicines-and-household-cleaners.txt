Medicines and household cleaners

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

MEDICINE IS NOT A SWEET

Some medicines and vitamins look like candy, and some supplements are made as gummies. Their appearance does not make them safe to eat like sweets. Do not take medicine on your own: a parent or another trusted caregiver must be the person giving it. An adult should explain what a medicine is for without calling it candy to persuade a child to take it. [MEDS, Keep young children safe; web.]

The rule includes vitamins and other supplements, not just prescription pills. A child finding something that looks like a sweet cannot reliably identify it from its color or shape. If a child may have swallowed a medicine or supplement that was not meant for them, tell an adult and seek medical help immediately, even when nobody is sure what happened. Waiting for obvious illness is not the response the source recommends. [MEDS, Keep young children safe and Medicine storage safety tips; web.]

ADULT STORAGE RESPONSIBILITIES

Adults should keep medicines and supplements out of young children's sight and reach. Put them away after each use, including between doses when a child is ill. Close a locking cap fully. Visitors' bags and coats can contain medicine too, so those also need safe storage. This is a task for the adults who control the medicines, not a reason for children to climb up and rearrange bottles. [MEDS, Medicine storage safety tips; web.]

CLEANING AND DISINFECTING ARE DIFFERENT

Cleaning uses soap or detergent, water and rubbing to remove dirt and germs. Disinfectants are products that kill germs left on surfaces. In most ordinary situations, cleaning with soap and water removes most germs; every surface does not automatically need bleach. A disinfectant for a table is not the same product as a hand cleaner. Use products only for their labeled purpose. [BLEACH, Overview and Definitions; HANDFAQ, Do wipes remove germs?; web.]

Never mix bleach or another disinfectant with other cleaners or disinfectants. The mixture can release dangerous fumes into the air. More products together do not mean better cleaning. An adult using a product must follow its directions and any instructions for ventilation and protective equipment. This topic does not give children mixing amounts or a disinfecting experiment. [BLEACH, Important and Before using bleach; web.]

NOT FOR SKIN OR DRINKING

Do not use surface-disinfecting wipes to clean your skin; they can irritate it. Drinking-water containers must not be old containers that held bleach, pesticides or other toxic chemicals. A chemical bottle being empty does not make it a drinking bottle. [HANDFAQ, Do wipes remove germs?; STORE, Don't use unsafe containers; web.]

This Knowledge Pack gives no medicine doses and no first-aid procedure for chemical exposure. Where supplies or professional help are unavailable, it does not invent a substitute chemical, medicine or treatment. The prevention advice above still matters, and a possible poisoning still needs help immediately. [MEDS, Medicine storage safety tips; web.]
