Using the toilet and keeping clean

Search terms: toilet, latrine, poop, poo, feces, bathroom, wipe, front to back, wash after toilet, sanitation, clean bottom, no toilet paper, no soap.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHY TOILET HYGIENE MATTERS

Feces is the health word for poop. Germs in feces can cause illness. They can get onto hands during a toilet visit and then travel to another person, food or a surface. This can happen at home as well as at school. Washing after every toilet visit helps interrupt that journey. A bathroom that looks clean does not remove the need to wash your hands. [HANDFAQ, Should I wash my hands after using the bathroom at home?; web.]

CLEAN HANDS AFTERWARD

After using the toilet, wet your hands with clean water and add soap. Rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers and under nails for at least 20 seconds. Rinse well and dry with a clean towel or air dry. Make washing part of the toilet visit, rather than leaving it until the next meal. Adults should help children who cannot manage these steps themselves. [HAND, How it works and Key times to wash hands; SCHOOLHAND, Promote hand hygiene; web.]

If there is no soap but there is water, rub your hands together under the water and dry them. This removes some germs, but less than soap and water. Use the cleanest water available and avoid water that may contain harmful chemicals. Sanitizer is not an equal replacement for washing after the toilet: it misses some germs that cause diarrhea, meaning loose or watery poop. [HANDFAQ, What if I have water but no soap to wash my hands? and What if the water is dirty or contaminated?; SCHOOLHAND, Know when and how to use hand sanitizer; web.]

WIPING AND PERSONAL WASHING

For someone with a vulva, wipe from the front toward the back when using toilet paper, rather than moving material from the bottom toward the vulva. The vulva is the outside genital area; the vagina is an internal passage. Wash the vulva and bottom daily, using only water to rinse the vulva. Do not put cleaning products inside the vagina. Its inside does not need washing out. [PERIOD, Other hygiene practices; web.]

During a period, wash hands before and after changing a pad or other menstrual product. Wrap used disposable pads and place them in a trash container; do not flush them down a toilet. For reusable products, the method of cleaning depends on the product. [PERIOD, Using menstrual products; web.]

WHEN THE FACILITIES ARE MISSING

For parents and teachers: provide washing water, soap and a way to dry hands that children can reach. This Knowledge Pack does not explain building a latrine, emptying a pit, replacing toilet paper or disposing of feces where no toilet exists. [SCHOOLHAND, Promote hand hygiene; web.]
