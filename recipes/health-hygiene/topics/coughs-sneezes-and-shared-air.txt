Coughs, sneezes and shared air

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

HOW BREATHING INFECTIONS SPREAD

Respiratory means to do with breathing. Respiratory infections can involve the nose, throat or lungs. When a person with an infection coughs or sneezes, germ-carrying drops can land on another person's eyes, nose or mouth, or on objects nearby. Some germs also travel in tiny airborne particles that can stay in the air and be breathed in. Not every infection uses exactly the same route. [COUGH, How it works and Respiratory Illnesses; GERM, Transmission; web.]

COVER COUGHS AND SNEEZES

Use a tissue to cover your mouth and nose when you cough or sneeze. Put the used tissue in the trash. If a tissue is unavailable, cough or sneeze into the bend of your elbow, rather than into your hands. Hands touch shared objects and food, so using them as the cover can help germs travel onward. The elbow habit is useful even when there is no box of tissues. [COUGH, Steps you can take; HAND, Why it's important; web.]

Clean your hands after coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose. Use soap and clean water, rubbing for at least 20 seconds, then rinse and dry. If soap and water are unavailable, a hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol can help. It does not remove all kinds of germs or harmful chemicals. Children who need help following its directions need adult supervision. [HAND, Key times to wash hands and How it works; SCHOOLHAND, Know when and how to use hand sanitizer and Prevent accidental poisoning; web.]

SHARED AIR MATTERS TOO

Covering coughs and washing hands reduce some routes of spread, but do not clean all the air in a room. Ventilation means bringing in outside air and moving indoor air out. For adults, opening windows can improve shared air when doing so creates no safety hazard. Outdoor activities are another option when suitable. Powered air-cleaning equipment is not required for opening a safe window or moving an activity outside. [GERM, Inhalation; AIR, Take Steps for Cleaner Air; web.]

An open window is not a guarantee that no infection will spread. Different germs and the amount of ventilation affect how particles travel. Adults need to consider whether a window or outdoor space is safe to use, rather than telling children to open every window regardless of the surroundings. If that option is unavailable, cough etiquette and hand hygiene still address other routes of spread. [GERM, Inhalation; AIR, Take Steps for Cleaner Air; COUGH, Recommendation; web.]

WHEN A CHILD IS ILL

A worsening cough or breathing symptoms that are not improving can be a reason to stay away from group learning while an adult assesses what is happening. This does not identify the illness or mean every cough is an infection: allergies can also cause symptoms. School attendance advice is not a substitute for medical help for serious breathing trouble. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; web.]
