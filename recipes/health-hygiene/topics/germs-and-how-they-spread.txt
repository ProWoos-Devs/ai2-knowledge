Germs and how they spread

Search terms: germs, microbes, bacteria, infection, catching illness, spread disease, dirty hands, coughing, sneezing, helpful germs, why wash, get sick.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHAT GERMS ARE

Germs is an everyday word for tiny things such as bacteria and viruses. Some can cause illness. Many microbes live in and on our bodies without harming us; some help keep us healthy. Having microbes on your skin does not mean you are dirty or bad. An infection happens when harmful germs enter the body, multiply and cause a reaction. Different germs spread in different ways. [GERM, opening paragraphs; web.]

HOW GERMS TRAVEL

Hands and objects can carry germs. Imagine someone touching a desk that has harmful germs on it, then rubbing their eyes. The germs may move from the desk to the hand and then into the body. Touching something does not always make a person ill: the germ needs a way into a person it can infect. Someone can also spread certain infections before they notice symptoms. [GERM, Contact and Source; web.]

Coughs and sneezes can send germ-carrying drops into another person's eyes, nose or mouth, or onto nearby objects. Some germs also travel in smaller particles that stay in the air and can be breathed in. That is why clean hands alone cannot prevent every respiratory infection. Respiratory means connected with breathing. Germs can also travel in contaminated food and water, or be carried by insects such as mosquitoes. [GERM, Transmission; web.]

BREAKING PART OF THE JOURNEY

Wash your hands with soap and water before eating and after using the toilet, touching animals or handling garbage. Rub all parts of your hands with the soap for at least 20 seconds, rinse and dry. Washing removes germs; it does not make your hands permanently free of them. Cleaning before the next meal matters even if you washed earlier. [HAND, Key times to wash hands and How it works; web.]

Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue when you cough or sneeze, put the used tissue in the trash and clean your hands. If there is no tissue, use the bend of your elbow instead of your hands. These habits reduce the spread of germs without needing to know the name of the illness. [COUGH, Steps you can take; HAND, Key times to wash hands; web.]

For parents and teachers: soap, water and a way to dry hands must be within children's reach. Some children need help with washing. An adult can also improve shared air by opening windows where that is safe or arranging activities outdoors. These habits lower risks; none is a promise that nobody will become ill.
