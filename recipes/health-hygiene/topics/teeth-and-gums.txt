Looking after teeth and gums

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHY TEETH NEED CLEANING

Teeth help you bite and chew. Dental plaque is a sticky film containing bacteria that forms on teeth. Some mouth bacteria use sugars and starches from food to make acids. Repeated acid attacks can damage enamel, the hard outer layer of a tooth. Early damage can sometimes be stopped or reversed, but a cavity is a permanent hole that needs a dentist's repair. If you have a toothache, tell an adult and ask for dental care. Brushing cannot repair a cavity. BRUSH TWICE A DAY

Use a brush with soft bristles. Brush your teeth twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, taking 2 minutes each time. Fluoride is a mineral that helps protect enamel. Point the bristles toward the edge where the tooth meets the gum and make small, gentle circles. Clean the outside, inside and chewing surfaces of the teeth, rather than only the teeth visible in a smile. Brush your tongue too. Hard scrubbing is not better cleaning. Replace a toothbrush when its bristles become worn. [BRUSH, Key Points and Toothbrushing Methods; TEETH, Brushing tips and Helpful Tips; web.]

Keep your toothbrush for your own use; do not share it. After each brushing, rinse it with safe water, stand it upright and let it dry in the air. Do not shut a damp brush inside a container. [BRUSH, Toothbrush Care; WATER, Overview; web.]

Spit out the toothpaste instead of swallowing it. For children aged 3 to 6, the adult puts a pea-sized amount on the brush; young children need supervision. A child may still need help with careful cleaning even after learning to hold a brush. [DECAY, Make sure your child brushes; web.]

CLEAN BETWEEN TEETH

Brush bristles do not do the whole job between teeth. Clean between teeth daily, using dental floss or another suitable dental cleaning tool. Ease floss gently between teeth; do not force it into the gums. Curve it around the side of a tooth and move it up and down. Ask a helping adult to show you if it is difficult. Use a tool intended for cleaning between teeth. [TEETH, Clean Between Your Teeth, Flossing tips and Helpful Tips; web.]

Plaque near the gums can make them red, swollen and quick to bleed. This early gum disease is called gingivitis. When caused by plaque buildup, it can usually be reversed with daily brushing and cleaning between teeth. Not every bleeding gum has the same cause. [TEETH, Clean Between Your Teeth; web.]

FOOD, WATER AND SUPPLIES

Limit sweets and sugary drinks, especially frequent sugary snacks. After brushing at bedtime, avoid foods or drinks containing sugar. Teeth need care even when they already have fillings. Use safe water for brushing; if a water supply may be unsafe, the same concern applies to brushing as to drinking. [DECAY, Tooth-friendly tips; TEETH, Brush Your Teeth; WATER, Overview; web.]

If toothpaste runs out, keep brushing and cleaning between your teeth: brushing removes plaque, while fluoride toothpaste adds protection against decay. Brushing without it does not supply that toothpaste's fluoride. This Knowledge Pack gives no homemade toothpaste recipe. [TEETH, Brush Your Teeth, Brushing tips and Clean Between Your Teeth; web.]

For parents and teachers: arrange dental care when it can be reached. Hardened plaque needs professional cleaning, and a cavity needs repair; ordinary brushing cannot do either job. [TEETH, Clean Between Your Teeth and Helpful Tips; DECAY, How does a cavity develop?; web.]
