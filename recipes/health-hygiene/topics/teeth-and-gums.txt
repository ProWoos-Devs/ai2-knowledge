Looking after teeth and gums

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHY TEETH NEED CLEANING

Teeth help you bite and chew. Dental plaque is a sticky film containing bacteria that forms on teeth. Some mouth bacteria use sugars and starches from food to make acids. Repeated acid attacks can damage enamel, the hard outer layer of a tooth. Early damage can sometimes be stopped or reversed, but a cavity is a permanent hole that needs a dentist's repair. Brushing is useful before a tooth starts to hurt. [TEETH, Overview and Brush Your Teeth; DECAY, What goes on inside our mouths all day? and How does a cavity develop?; web.]

BRUSH TWICE A DAY

Brush your teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste. Fluoride is a mineral that helps protect enamel. Point the bristles toward the edge where the tooth meets the gum and make small, gentle circles. Clean the outside, inside and chewing surfaces of the teeth, rather than only the teeth visible in a smile. Brush your tongue too. Hard scrubbing is not better cleaning. Replace a toothbrush when its bristles become worn. [TEETH, Brushing tips and Helpful Tips; web.]

Spit out the toothpaste instead of swallowing it. For children aged 3 to 6, the adult puts a pea-sized amount on the brush; young children need supervision. A child may still need help with careful cleaning even after learning to hold a brush. This Knowledge Pack is for ages 6 to 12; toothpaste advice for babies should not be guessed from these instructions. [DECAY, Make sure your child brushes; web.]

CLEAN BETWEEN TEETH

Brush bristles do not do the whole job between teeth. Clean between teeth daily, using dental floss or another suitable dental cleaning tool. Ease floss gently between teeth; do not force it into the gums. Curve it around the side of a tooth and move it up and down. Ask a helping adult to show you if it is difficult. Use a tool intended for cleaning between teeth. [TEETH, Clean Between Your Teeth, Flossing tips and Helpful Tips; web.]

FOOD, WATER AND SUPPLIES

Limit sweets and sugary drinks, especially frequent sugary snacks. After brushing at bedtime, avoid foods or drinks containing sugar. Teeth need care even when they already have fillings. Use safe water for brushing; if a water supply may be unsafe, the same concern applies to brushing as to drinking. [DECAY, Tooth-friendly tips; TEETH, Brush Your Teeth; WATER, Overview; web.]

For parents and teachers: arrange dental care when it can be reached. Hardened plaque and cavities cannot be removed by ordinary brushing at home. These sources give no replacement for fluoride toothpaste when it has run out, and this topic supplies no homemade toothpaste or method of filling a tooth.
