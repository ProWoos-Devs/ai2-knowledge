Washing hands with soap

Search terms: wash hands, handwashing, soap, twenty seconds, clean hands, sanitizer, no soap, no tap, water shortage, dirty fingernails, before eating, after toilet.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

WHEN TO WASH

Wash your hands before and after eating; before, during and after preparing food; and after using the toilet. Also wash after blowing your nose, coughing or sneezing, handling garbage, or touching animals, animal food or animal waste. Harmful germs can move from unwashed hands to food or to your eyes, nose and mouth. Hands can carry germs even when they do not look muddy. [HAND, Why it's important and Key times to wash hands; web.]

Wash after changing a diaper or cleaning a child who has used the toilet. Wash before and after helping care for someone with vomiting or diarrhea, and before and after treating a cut or wound. These are washing times for anyone involved in care; they are not instructions for children to treat injuries on their own. [HAND, Key times to wash hands; web.]

SOAP, RUB, RINSE, DRY

1. Wet your hands with clean, running water. Warm or cold water is suitable. Turn off the tap while you rub, if there is a tap.
2. Add plain soap and rub your hands together to make a lather. Rub your palms, the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.
3. Keep scrubbing for at least 20 seconds.
4. Rinse the soap off under clean, running water.
5. Dry your hands completely with a clean towel or air dry. Germs spread more easily from wet hands. Bar soap and liquid soap both work; an antibacterial label is not needed. [HAND, How it works; HANDFAQ, Is bar soap better than liquid soap? and Should I dry my hands using a paper towel or an air dryer?; web.]

Washing removes germs and dirt. Water hot enough to kill germs on its own can scald your skin. A clean reusable towel is suitable; replace it when dirty and before it develops mildew from staying damp. [HANDFAQ, Is it better to use warm water or cold water? and Should I reuse a towel to dry my hands at home?; web.]

WHEN SUPPLIES ARE MISSING

If soap is missing but water is available, rub your hands together under the water, then dry them. This rinses away some germs, but works less well than washing with soap. Use the cleanest water available; avoid cloudy water or water that may contain harmful chemicals. Shared, already-used washing water can pass germs onto hands. [HANDFAQ, What if I have water but no soap to wash my hands? and What if the water is dirty or contaminated?; web.]

If soap and water are unavailable, a hand sanitizer containing at least 60% alcohol can help. Use enough sanitizer to cover all hand surfaces. Rub your hands and fingers together until completely dry, usually about 20 seconds. Do not rinse or wipe it off before it dries. It does not remove every germ or harmful chemical and works less well on dirty or greasy hands. Soap and water remain especially important after the toilet and when hands are visibly dirty. Children who cannot follow the directions need adult help using sanitizer. Do not swallow it or make your own. [SANITIZER, How it works, Dos and Don'ts; SCHOOLHAND, Know when and how to use hand sanitizer and Prevent accidental poisoning; HANDFAQ, Should I make my own hand sanitizer?; web.]
