When you feel ill

Search terms: feel ill, sick, unwell, fever, vomiting, diarrhea, tummy ache, cough, tell an adult, stay home, trouble breathing, cannot wake, need help, no doctor.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

DESCRIBE WHAT IS HAPPENING

Symptoms are changes you notice when you feel unwell. They can include feeling feverish, a cough, vomiting, loose or watery poop called diarrhea, or unusual tiredness. A child and a helping adult may not know which illness is causing them. Describe what you notice to a parent, teacher or other trusted caregiver instead of having to name a disease first. The same symptom can occur with more than one illness. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; FLU, Symptoms and Cold versus flu; web.]

Useful details include whether you have vomited repeatedly, whether poop contains blood or causes accidents, and whether a cough is getting worse rather than better. These are among the signs the school guidance considers when deciding whether a child should stay away from a group. They are not a way to decide that every other symptom is harmless. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; web.]

WHEN HELP IS URGENT

CDC lists trouble breathing, bluish lips or face, not being alert or interacting when awake, and seizures as emergency warning signs for flu complications in children. Seek medical help right away for these signs. A seizure is a temporary change in brain activity; it can involve shaking or a loss of awareness, but may look different. These signs are not a diagnosis of flu. [FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; SEIZURE, Overview and Signs and symptoms; web.]

Dehydration means the body has lost too much fluid. CDC's emergency warning signs for children include not urinating for 8 hours, a dry mouth or no tears when crying. Seek medical help right away for these signs rather than treating them only as reasons to miss school. The list is not complete: other severe or concerning symptoms also need assessment. [FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; web.]

AVOID SPREADING AN INFECTION

An adult should keep a child away from group learning when illness prevents comfortable participation or the child's care needs cannot be met there. Wash hands after the toilet and after coughing, sneezing or blowing the nose. Cover coughs with a tissue or elbow. These habits help protect other people even when the illness has no known name. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; HAND, Key times to wash hands; COUGH, Steps you can take; web.]

Returning depends on the illness and recovery, not only on a child wanting to join a game. For example, CDC's general school guidance requires at least 24 hours without a fever and without fever-reducing medicine. Bloody diarrhea or a fever with a new rash needs medical assessment before returning. Those school rules are not medical clearance for every illness. When the required medical assessment cannot be reached, this text cannot declare it safe to return. [SICK, Return to School; web.]

MEDICINES AND UNAVAILABLE HELP

Do not take a medicine on your own. A trusted caregiver must handle it. If a child may have swallowed an unintended medicine, get help immediately, even if no symptoms have appeared. [MEDS, Keep young children safe; web.]

This topic gives no diagnosis, dose or home-treatment recipe. If professional help cannot be reached, the urgent signs remain urgent; an offline text cannot make those conditions safe or replace the treatment they may need.
