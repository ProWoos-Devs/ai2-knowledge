When you feel ill

Search terms: feel ill, sick, unwell, fever, vomiting, diarrhea, tummy ache, cough, tell an adult, trouble breathing, cannot wake, blood in poop, cannot drink, need help, no doctor.

Written for AI-2. Directed to children, parents and teachers.

DESCRIBE WHAT IS HAPPENING

Symptoms are changes you notice when you feel unwell. Tell a parent, teacher or trusted caregiver what you notice: feeling feverish, coughing, vomiting, loose or watery poop called diarrhea, unusual tiredness or a tummy ache. A tummy ache is pain in your belly. It can occur with vomiting or diarrhea, but those symptoms do not tell you which illness you have. You do not have to name a disease before asking for help. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; FLU, Symptoms; NORO, Signs and symptoms; web.]

DRINKING DURING ILLNESS

With diarrhea or vomiting, keep drinking safe fluids to replace what your body loses and help prevent dehydration; safe water helps, and properly prepared oral rehydration solution also replaces lost salts. [DIARRHEA, What to do if you or your family are ill with diarrhea; NORO, Drink plenty of liquids; web.]

Seek medical help right away if a child with vomiting or diarrhea cannot drink enough to replace losses or does not improve after drinking oral rehydration solution. Also seek medical help right away if a child with vomiting or diarrhea was born prematurely (too early) or has a history of other medical conditions. [GASTRO, Seek care right away, Infants and children; web.]

Seek medical help right away for a child with vomiting or diarrhea who has a change in thinking or behavior, such as unusual irritability (being easily upset) or little energy; high fever in an older child; frequent loose stools; vomiting often; diarrhea lasting more than a day; severe belly or rectal pain; or poop containing blood or pus, or looking black and sticky. [GASTRO, Seek care right away, Infants and children; web.]

Rectal pain means pain in the rectum, the last part of the large intestine, where poop waits before leaving the body. Pus is a thick fluid that looks yellow or white. [GASTRO, linked NIDDK Dictionary definitions, rectum and pus; web.]

With vomiting or diarrhea, thirst, urinating less than usual, little energy, a dry mouth, no tears when crying, sunken eyes or cheeks, or skin that does not quickly flatten back to normal after a pinch is released can be signs of dehydration. Seek medical help right away for these signs; do not wait for 8 hours without urinating. [GASTRO, What are the symptoms of dehydration? and Seek care right away; web.]

WHEN HELP IS URGENT

For a child with fast breathing, trouble breathing, ribs pulling in with each breath, bluish lips or face, chest pain, a seizure, or not being alert or responding to others while awake: tell an adult and seek medical help right away. CDC lists these as emergency warning signs of flu complications; they do not prove that an illness is flu. [FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications, In children; web.]

Also seek medical help right away for muscle pain so severe a child refuses to walk; fever above 40 °C that is not controlled by fever-reducing medicine; fever or cough that improves but then returns or worsens; or a long-term medical condition getting worse. These are more of CDC's emergency warning signs for children. [FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications, In children; web.]

Dehydration means losing too much body fluid. Not urinating for 8 hours, a dry mouth or no tears when crying also need medical help right away. A seizure is a temporary change in brain activity that can involve shaking or loss of awareness. Other severe or worrying symptoms need medical assessment too. [FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; SEIZURE, Overview and Signs and symptoms; web.]

MEDICINES AND UNAVAILABLE HELP

Do not choose a medicine or dose without instructions for you. Usually a caregiver gives your medicine. If your health professional and caregiver have agreed that you can use a particular prescribed medicine yourself and taught you how, follow that personal plan. This Knowledge Pack does not replace it. If a child might have taken an unintended medicine, vitamin or supplement, get help immediately, even if you are not completely sure. [ASTHMA pp. 13–14; MEDS, Keep young children safe; web.]

This Knowledge Pack gives no medicine doses or rehydration recipes. If medical help cannot be reached, the urgent signs still need treatment that these texts cannot provide.

STAYING HOME IS A SEPARATE QUESTION

Needing medical help and staying home from group learning are separate questions. The topic “Staying home and returning to group learning” explains the stay-home list and return criteria for parents and teachers. Being at home does not replace getting help for urgent signs. [SICK, Staying Home When Sick; FLU, Emergency Warning Signs of Flu Complications; web.]
